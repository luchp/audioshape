\abstract{%
Selecting a sealed-box bass driver for a given room and level target is
usually done by heuristics such as the efficiency-bandwidth product (EBP).
We derive, from the lumped electromechanical model of a sealed
direct-radiator loudspeaker, the maximum acoustic output as a function of
frequency subject to linear excursion and continuous coil dissipation, with
an unconstrained amplifier. Frequency-domain and time-domain (burst)
analyses are shown to yield the same two figures of merit. The complete
sealed-box selection problem reduces to four datasheet-computable
invariants -- displacement volume, acoustic power, a corner-placement
parameter, and an upper validity frequency -- plus one regime-boundary
frequency. We show EBP is a legitimate enclosure-type screen but not an
absolute ranking, and that a motor-materials bound forbids one driver from
maximizing both the excursion and dissipation figures of merit at once. We
further show that every non-linearly-correctable distortion mechanism --
motor/suspension nonlinearity, Doppler frequency modulation, and box-spring
distortion -- is monotone in the same headroom ratios, so
distortion-referenced selection reduces to headroom maximization. Closing
the low end with the room's own pressure-zone gain gives an explicit
displacement-volume sizing rule and corner-frequency target, and we show
why, under this paper's DSP-corrected, room-integrated premise, sealed is
the stronger enclosure choice over vented. A worked numerical
example and an open-source, unit-tested reference implementation
accompany the results.}
