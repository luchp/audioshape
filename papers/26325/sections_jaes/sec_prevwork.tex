\section{Previous work}
\label{sec:prevwork}

Thiele/Small alignment theory and the sealed-box alignment line
\eqref{eq:alignment} \cite{small1972} are the common starting point;
nothing here revisits that theory. 
The efficiency-bandwidth product $EBP\equiv F_s/\Qes$ is a widely 
used single number for sealed-vs-vented screening, 
$EBP\lesssim50$ suggesting sealed, $\gtrsim100$ vented \cite{dickason2006}. 
As \cref{sec:invariants} shows, $EBP$ is a genuine motor/mass ratio 
\eqref{eq:sebound}: a legitimate enclosure-\emph{type} screen, 
but it says nothing about \emph{how much} SPL or how little
distortion either choice delivers once the type is fixed. Below, $EBP$
enters only through the regime-boundary frequency $f_x$ \eqref{eq:fx} and
the corner rate \eqref{eq:cornerrate}, never as a standalone ranking.

A few related shorthands are corrected in passing, since later sections
rely on the corrected versions silently. Overshoot rules of thumb quote
$1$--$2\%$ for $\Qtc=0.55$; the exact value is $M_p=0.11\%$,
and $M_p\le1\%$ holds up to $\Qtc\le0.61$.
``Excursion grows as $1/f^2$ going down'' holds only above the sealed
corner $F_c$; below it, excursion at fixed drive saturates
\eqref{eq:PL}. Finally, ``given $\Qtc$ and a frequency, what SPL can this
driver make'' is ill-posed: the excursion-limited ceiling \eqref{eq:SPLL}
does not depend on $\Qtc$ (\cref{cor:onlyVd}); $\Qtc$ governs corner
placement and settling only.

Neither the $EBP$ screen nor these shorthands produces a datasheet-computable
ranking that is (a)~derived rather than guessed, (b)~referenced to
non-correctable distortion, and (c)~accounts for room gain. Supplying that
ranking is the subject of the rest of this paper.
