\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Choosing bass drivers for a specific loudspeaker project is normally done
before anything can be measured. The information available at that moment
is the manufacturer's published Thiele/Small and rating fields, and the
decision rules commonly applied to them---most prominently the
efficiency-bandwidth product $EBP=F_s/\Qes$ \cite{dickason2006}---are
undocumented in the sense that they do not state the operating point, the
enclosure, the room, the target level, or the amplifier at which they are
supposed to hold.

This Engineering Report replaces that step, for one deliberately narrow
architecture, with an explicit procedure assembled from established
first-principles relations. The procedure is a \emph{pre-purchase}
screening and ranking method operating on public datasheet fields alone. It
is not a substitute for post-purchase measurement of a physical driver.

\subsection{Application and architecture}
\label{sec:intro-arch}

The target system, sketched in \cref{fig:architecture}, is fixed
throughout:

\begin{itemize}
\item a compact primary listening area---a prepared living-room couch
	area---represented by a declared reference position or a small declared
	set of measurement positions, not by a mathematical point;
\item half-space (soffit-wall) mounting of all bass sources;
\item one shared \emph{mono} low-bass manifold covering
	$f_1=\SI{15}{\hertz}$ to $f_{\mathrm{sp}}=\SI{80}{\hertz}$, built from
	an even number of nominally identical drivers arranged as mechanically
	opposed pairs, permitting first-order reaction-force cancellation under
	matched drive and mounting;
\item \emph{independent} left and right upper-bass channels covering
	$f_{\mathrm{sp}}$ to $f_2=\SI{250}{\hertz}$, each carrying its own
	program material;
\item one dedicated amplifier channel per physical driver, with declared
	voltage, current, continuous-power, and burst-power limits;
\item finite-band DSP crossover, delay, and magnitude/phase correction
	across the design band.
\end{itemize}

Target levels are maximum-output design tests---short, occasional
peaks---not continuous exposure levels. The mono target is the
\emph{total} level produced by the complete manifold at the listening
distance; the upper-bass target applies to \emph{each} stereo channel
separately. Because the two stereo channels reproduce independent program
material, no summing credit is taken between them.

\subsection{What the architecture is intended to buy}
\label{sec:intro-fidelity}

The objective is high fidelity to the recorded two-channel scene over the
primary listening area: stable localization and image focus, accurate
direct- and early-field timbre, controlled low-frequency summation, and
coherent temporal integration across the crossover. In small rooms these
attributes depend strongly on the direct sound and the early reflection
pattern \cite{toole2006,bech1998}, on the relative phase and delay of
noncoincident sources \cite{linkwitz1976}, and on group-delay error
remaining below audibility bounds \cite{blauert1978}---all of which a
compact, prepared, soffit-mounted, DSP-aligned system can control over a
small area.

This is deliberately \emph{not} a claim of greater listener envelopment, nor
of superiority over multi-seat or diffuse-field designs. Envelopment is a
separate attribute governed largely by late lateral energy
\cite{bradley1995,rumsey2002} and is commonly better served by multichannel
or deliberately diffuse reproduction \cite{francombe2017}. The present
architecture trades room-wide coverage and off-area robustness---the
problem addressed by distributed multiple-subwoofer optimization
\cite{welti2006}---for control over one small area. This report evaluates
that trade objectively; it contains no controlled subjective comparison.

\subsection{Contribution}
\label{sec:intro-contribution}

The physical relations used here are established prior results
(\cref{tab:prior-art}). The contribution of this report is their
integration into a decision procedure with a declared policy:

\begin{enumerate}
\item exact displacement- and dissipation-limited output ceilings, and the
	sealed alignment line, assembled into role-specific feasibility gates
	under one declared couch-area reference basis
	(\cref{sec:model,sec:freq});
\item explicit finite-amplifier gates on voltage, current, continuous power,
	and burst power for every physical driver, evaluated across the complete
	role band (\cref{sec:freq});
\item transient demand obtained by numerically solving the sealed-box
	equation for declared bursts and start phases, rather than from a
	constant burst factor (\cref{sec:time});
\item room-specific demand from a leaky pressure-zone model with declared
	sensitivity to the leakage corner (\cref{sec:room});
\item separately reported nonlinear-risk indicators and a declared,
	non-compensatory ranking policy---hard gates, role-specific Pareto
	fronts, then a role-specific lexicographic order
	(\cref{sec:risk,sec:procedure});
\item a reproducible implementation, aggregate evidence from a frozen
	candidate population under a documented private-data boundary, and a
	worked example built from public manufacturer datasheets
	(\cref{sec:procedure,app:example}).
\end{enumerate}

\Cref{sec:prevwork} positions this against prior work, \cref{sec:scope}
states the scope contract, and \cref{sec:conclusion} records what the
method does not establish. \Cref{app:vented} explains, within this
application, why sealed loading was chosen.
