\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Choosing bass drivers for a specific loudspeaker project is normally done
before anything can be measured. The information available at that moment
is the manufacturer's published Thiele/Small and rating fields, and the
decision rules commonly applied to them---most prominently the
efficiency-bandwidth product $EBP=F_s/\Qes$ \cite{dickason2006}---are
undocumented in the sense that they do not state the operating point, the
enclosure, the room, the target level, or the amplifier at which they are
supposed to hold.

This Engineering Report replaces that step, for a deliberately narrow
application family, with an explicit procedure assembled from established
first-principles relations. The procedure is a \emph{pre-purchase}
screening and ranking method operating on public datasheet fields alone.
Room, frequencies, targets, amplifier and packaging limits are user inputs;
driver model, diameter, unit count, enclosure and architecture status are
outputs. The numerical system used in the figures is an illustrative
scenario, not an immutable prescription. The method is not a substitute for
post-purchase measurement of a physical driver.

\subsection{Application and architecture}
\label{sec:intro-arch}

The target application and selectable architecture, sketched in
\cref{fig:architecture}, are:

\begin{itemize}
\item a compact primary listening area---for example a prepared living-room
	couch or mastering-desk area---represented by a declared reference
	position or a small declared set of measurement positions, not by a
	mathematical point;
\item half-space (soffit-wall) mounting of all bass sources;
\item user-selected $f_1$, crossover $f_{\mathrm{sp}}$, upper edge $f_2$,
	and maximum-output targets;
\item a primary two-role candidate: one shared \emph{mono} low-bass source
	below $f_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and \emph{independent} left and right upper-bass
	channels above it, each carrying its own programme material;
\item an explicitly tested baseline in which the same full-band woofer
	array in each stereo channel carries the shared mono bass below
	$f_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and independent programme above it;
\item when the mono source is a compact manifold, an even number of
	nominally identical drivers in mechanically opposed pairs and a declared
	maximum nominal crossover; manifold cavity and path-length behaviour are
	not inferred from Thiele/Small data;
\item one dedicated amplifier channel per physical driver, with declared
	voltage, current, continuous-power, and burst-power limits;
\item finite-band DSP crossover, delay, and magnitude/phase correction
	across the design band.
\end{itemize}

The canonical evidence uses \SIrange{15}{250}{\hertz}, an
\SI{80}{\hertz} nominal crossover, \SI{110}{dB} total low bass and
\SI{105}{dB} per upper channel. These are maximum-output tests---short,
occasional peaks---not continuous exposure levels. Because the upper stereo
channels reproduce independent programme, no summing credit is taken
between them. The full-band baseline receives a favourable \SI{6}{dB}
coherent mono-bass summation credit at the reference position, with a
\SI{3}{dB} sensitivity check.

\subsection{Frontal recorded-scene precision and its boundary}
\label{sec:intro-fidelity}

The objective is \emph{frontal recorded-scene precision}: stable source
location, width and image focus; accurate direct- and early-field timbre;
controlled low-frequency summation; and coherent crossover timing over the
primary area. It is an operational design term for cues encoded in the
two-channel programme under a stated loudspeaker geometry, not a claim to
reconstruct one unique original sound field. These attributes depend on
direct sound and early reflections \cite{toole2006,bech1998}, relative
phase and delay of noncoincident sources \cite{linkwitz1976}, and
frequency- and programme-dependent group-delay audibility
\cite{blauert1978}. With added latency, finite-band DSP can bring measured
minimum-phase low-frequency components toward a common linear-phase target
over the chosen small area. Nonminimum-phase errors, deep spatial nulls,
filter length and position dependence prevent exact room inversion
\cite{neely1979,cecchi2018}.

Listener envelopment is a separate attribute governed mainly by the level,
direction and timing of later-arriving energy
\cite{bradley1995,rumsey2002,soulodre2003}. A separately adjustable, band-limited and
delayed low-correlation ambience layer may therefore be added after the
front system is aligned, but preservation of frontal imaging remains
conditional on its level, spectrum, delay, direction and correlation
\cite{kendall1995,litovsky1999}. The
reverse design sequence is less direct: equalization can change drive and
delay, but cannot by itself move acoustic centres, change directivity,
remove a reflection at its physical origin, or make different seat
transfers identical \cite{neely1979,elliott1989,welti2006,cecchi2018}. This is an engineering
asymmetry, not an impossibility theorem or a claim that diffuse or
multichannel systems cannot image accurately.

A coherent coaxial main system above $f_2$ is a compatible extension.
Relieving its low-frequency cone of bass and midbass reduces that cone's
excursion and hence excitation of displacement-dependent nonlinearities.
Any additional reduction of high-frequency modulation when the cone also
forms a coaxial waveguide is topology dependent and requires multitone and
off-axis verification; it is not assumed by the present ranking.

\subsection{Contribution}
\label{sec:intro-contribution}

The physical relations used here are established prior results
(\cref{tab:prior-art}). The contribution of this report is their
integration into a decision procedure with a declared policy:

\begin{enumerate}
\item exact displacement- and dissipation-limited output ceilings, and the
	sealed alignment line, assembled into role-specific feasibility gates
	under one declared listening-area reference basis
	(\cref{sec:model,sec:freq});
\item explicit finite-amplifier gates on voltage, current, continuous power,
	and burst power for every physical driver, evaluated across the complete
	role band (\cref{sec:freq});
\item transient demand obtained by numerically solving the sealed-box
	equation for declared bursts and start phases, rather than from a
	constant burst factor (\cref{sec:time});
\item room-specific demand from a leaky pressure-zone model with declared
	sensitivity to the leakage corner (\cref{sec:room});
\item separately reported nonlinear-risk indicators and a declared,
	non-compensatory ranking policy---hard gates, role-specific Pareto
	fronts, then a role-specific lexicographic order
	(\cref{sec:risk,sec:procedure});
\item crossover sensitivity and a Pareto comparison of separate-radiator
	and stereo full-band architectures over user-variable room,
	$f_{\mathrm{sp}}$, $f_2$, and SPL inputs, without converting architecture tradeoffs
	into one weighted score;
\item a reproducible implementation, aggregate evidence from a frozen
	candidate population under a documented private-data boundary, and a
	worked example built from public manufacturer datasheets
	(\cref{sec:procedure,app:example}).
\end{enumerate}

\Cref{sec:prevwork} positions this against prior work, \cref{sec:scope}
states the scope contract, and \cref{sec:conclusion} records what the
method does not establish. \Cref{app:vented} explains, within this
application, why sealed loading was chosen.
