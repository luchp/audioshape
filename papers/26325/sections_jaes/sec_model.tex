\section{Model and exact transfer relations}
\label{sec:model}

Terminal voltage $e(t)$, current $i(t)$, cone displacement $x(t)$. Electrical
side (A2): $e=\Re i+\Bl\dot{x}$; force $F=\Bl\,i$. With total stiffness
$k_t=1/\Cms+\rho_0c^2\Sd^2/\Vb=(1+\alpha)/\Cms$, $\alpha\equiv\Vas/\Vb$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \ddot{x}+\sigma\dot{x}+\wc^2 x=\frac{\Bl}{\Re \Mms}\,e(t)\ }
\label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
with
$\sigma\equiv\sm+\se$, $\sm\equiv\Rms/\Mms$, $\se\equiv\Bl^2/(\Re \Mms)$, and
$\wc^2\equiv k_t/\Mms$.
Free-air: $\ws=2\pi F_s=\sqrt{1/(\Mms\Cms)}$, $\Qes=\ws/\se$, $\Qms=\ws/\sm$,
$\Qts=\ws/\sigma$, $EBP\equiv F_s/\Qes=\se/2\pi$. In-box: $\wc=\ws\sqrt{1+\alpha}$,
$\Qtc=\wc/\sigma$.

\begin{proposition}[Alignment line; box invariance of $\sigma$ --- after \cite{small1972}]
\label{prop:invariance}
$\sigma$ contains no stiffness, hence is enclosure-independent:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \frac{\wc}{\Qtc}=\frac{\ws}{\Qts}=\sigma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_c = \Qtc\,\frac{F_s}{\Qts}\ }
\label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
with $\Vb=\Vas/((\Qtc/\Qts)^2-1)$ and $\Qtc>\Qts$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\sigma=(\Rms+\Bl^2/\Re)/\Mms$; the box adds only $k_t$, so
$\wc/\Qtc=\sigma=\ws/\Qts$ and $F_c/F_s=\sqrt{1+\alpha}=\Qtc/\Qts$.
\end{proof}

A sealed driver owns one free parameter, position along $\wc=\Qtc\sigma$; the
driver constant
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}=\frac{F_s}{\Qts}=EBP\left(1+\frac{\Qes}{\Qms}\right)\approx EBP
\label{eq:cornerrate}
\end{equation}
is the \emph{corner rate}: the rigorous content of $EBP$ is where the corner
lands for a chosen $\Qtc$, not output capability.

Steady sine, phasor amplitudes ($\hat{x}$ peak):
\begin{align}
\hat{x}(\omega)&=\frac{\Bl}{\Re\Mms}\,
\frac{\hat{e}}{\sqrt{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sigma^2\omega^2}},
\label{eq:xofe}\\
\hat{\imath}(\omega)&=\frac{\Mms}{\Bl}\,
\hat{x}\,\sqrt{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2},
\label{eq:iofx}\\
\hat{p}(\omega)&=\frac{\rho_0\Sd\,\omega^2\hat{x}}{2\pi r}\qquad\text{(A3)}.
\label{eq:pofx}
\end{align}
Only mechanical damping $\sm$ enters \eqref{eq:iofx}; electrical damping
$\se$ appears in \eqref{eq:xofe} (voltage drive) but not in the
current-to-displacement relation.
