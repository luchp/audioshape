\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a compact prepared couch or mastering area, this report assembles
established sealed-box relations into a parameterized, reproducible
pre-purchase procedure operating on public datasheet fields. Room,
frequencies, levels, electronics, enclosure, allowed sizes/counts and any
manifold ceiling are inputs; driver model, diameter, count, alignment,
architecture status and margins are outputs.

\textbf{What is exact within the model.} The sealed-box equation, the
alignment line \cref{eq:alignment}, the displacement- and
dissipation-limited ceilings \cref{eq:SPLL,eq:SPLT}, their crossover
\cref{eq:fx}, and the steady electrical requirements
\cref{eq:Ereq,eq:Ireq,eq:Preq} follow from the stated lumped model. The
transient factor \cref{eq:ctr} is obtained numerically from the same model
for a declared waveform family. The overhung-motor relation
\cref{eq:motor-scaling} is exact only under its explicitly idealized flux,
gap, conductor and coil-mass assumptions; it is motivation for a
comparison, never a gate or universal bound.

\textbf{What is empirical.} In the frozen candidate population and
canonical data-complete comparison, 119 unique low-bass records and 301
upper-bass records are feasible at one or more allowed counts, whereas no
stereo full-band record is feasible. The split architecture therefore
occupies the canonical system Pareto frontier. Permitting missing
inductance produces one unresolved full-band survivor. The public
Dayton/Faital worked pair is feasible from 70 to \SI{80}{\hertz};
\SI{80}{\hertz} is selected only as the highest nominal point inside the
declared manifold band. Across the wider factorial, full-band systems share
fronts at the lower targets but disappear at \SI{115}{dB}; the manifold
ceiling can also make the full-band architecture the only admissible option
at higher crossovers. These are observations about the stated population,
scenario envelope and packaging limits, not proof that one driver model can
never serve both roles.

\textbf{What is policy.} The hard gates, Pareto objectives, 0.80 preferred
excursion margin, Doppler budget, preferred $\Qtc^\ast$, $4\Vas$
per-driver cap, manifold ceiling, allowed size/count ranges and
lexicographic driver orders are declared engineering choices. Enclosure
volume and driver count remain separate system objectives rather than
being hidden in a weighted score.

\textbf{What is application-specific.} The worked system meets the
declared maximum-output targets within every gate for the stated room,
electronics and bands. Its aim is frontal recorded-scene precision over a
small area. Listener envelopment is a separate attribute; an ambience
layer may be added conditionally, but its imaging effect depends on level,
spectrum, delay, direction and correlation.

\textbf{Limitations.} The driver model is small-signal and LTI; nonlinear
indicators are proxies, not predicted distortion; $\Xmax$, $\Pmax$ and
$\Le$ conventions differ between manufacturers; the pressure-zone model
is a low-order description over one band and one small area; and the
Thiele/Small model does not predict manifold cavity modes, unequal paths or
compression. DSP correction is finite-band, cannot remove nonlinear
residuals or replace source geometry and room treatment, and no controlled
subjective evaluation is reported. Robustness analysis validates decision
stability, not the physical correctness of the proxies.

The next useful experiment is a controlled system comparison: build
representative split and full-band candidates from adjacent Pareto fronts,
measure $Bl(x)$, $K_{\mathrm{ms}}(x)$, burst headroom, modulation
residuals, compression, manifold transfer, off-axis behaviour and spatial
crossover integration, and test whether the indicator vector predicts the
measured ordering.
