\section{Non-correctable distortion: scaling laws and the distortion-referenced criterion}
\label{sec:distortion}

The invariants above rank drivers by \emph{output}. But a DSP-aligned system
(\cref{sec:assumptions}) can move gain, phase, and group delay around almost for
free, so the only distortion that should decide a ranking is the kind no
filter can remove.

\begin{proposition}[FIR correctability boundary]
	\label{prop:LTI}
	(i)~In-band, the sealed-box transfer function is minimum-phase, so a
		causal, stable LTI pre-filter $G\approx H^{-1}$ exists on any band: 
		magnitude, phase, group delay, corner sag, and ring-down tail 
		are all exactly	correctable (at the drive costs \eqref{eq:SPLL}, \eqref{eq:EQtax}).
	(ii)~No LTI $G$ reduces the nonlinear residual $\mathcal{N}$ for all program:
		if it did, scaling the input would force $\mathcal N$ linear on the signal
		space --- contradiction unless $\mathcal N\equiv0$.
\end{proposition}

Three independent, first-principles mechanisms all reduce to the same
headroom ranking, which is the central result of this section.

\textbf{(1) Displacement-driven nonlinearity.} 
Expanding motor and suspension nonlinearity to leading Taylor order (A10) 
and anchoring at the distortion-defined $\Xmax$ (A7, $D(\Xmax)\approx10\%$) 
gives, with utilization $\xi_x\equiv V_{\mathrm{dem}}/\Vd$,

\begin{equation}
	\boxed{\ D(\xi_x)\approx d_2\,\xi_x+d_3\,\xi_x^{2},\quad d_2+d_3\approx0.1\ }.
	\label{eq:HDscale}
\end{equation}

Since $d_2,d_3\ge0$, this bounds $D$ between its two limiting orders,
$0.1\,\xi_x^{2}\le D\le0.1\,\xi_x$. 

At fixed target this distortion class is monotone in the headroom on the
displacement ceiling \eqref{eq:SPLL}: sorting by it is sorting by $\Vd$. 
A distortion budget $D^\ast$ converts to a utilization cap
$\xi^\ast=D^\ast/0.1$ (even-order) or $\sqrt{D^\ast/0.1}$ (symmetric); 
e.g. $D^\ast=3\%\Rightarrow\xi^\ast=0.3$--$0.55$, i.e.\ $\Vd$ must be $2$--$3\times$
the bare SPL-ceiling requirement --- this factor, not the SPL math, sizes
the sub. $D^\ast$ is a selection ceiling at the brief reference-level target
(exposure-limited to about a minute), not a continuous operating point; the
figures also plot the same total $20$~dB down as a normal-listening proxy,
typically far under budget.
(Per A10, this leading-order expansion sizes headroom correctly as a ranking law; 
it is not a $\pm1$\,dB THD predictor.)

\textbf{(2) Doppler (FM) distortion, the second use of area.} 
A component at $f_2$ riding on a cone at position $x_1(t)$ acquires phase modulation 
index $m=2\pi f_2X_1/c$, giving sidebands
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{\ \frac{J_1(m)}{J_0(m)} \approx 
			 \frac{\pi f_2}{c}\cdot\frac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f_1)}{\Sd}\ }.
	\label{eq:doppler}
\end{equation}

This is FM, maximally non-LTI-correctable; at fixed output it scales as
$1/\Sd$ and is independent of $\Xmax$, so among equal-$\Vd$ drivers the
larger cone is strictly cleaner --- area enters selection a \emph{second}
time. It vanishes identically under band splitting, since $f_2$ no longer
rides on the cone doing $X_1$: \eqref{eq:doppler} evaluated at the top of a
candidate band is the quantitative split criterion, provided the crossover
itself is steep enough (e.g.\ a linear-phase FIR) that out-of-band
excursion does not intermodulate back in --- implicit in the
unconstrained-DSP premise (A6), but worth stating explicitly.

\textbf{(3) Sealed-box air-spring distortion.} Adiabatic box compression is
an asymmetric spring; weighting by the box's share of total stiffness
(\cref{prop:invariance}) gives
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{\ \mathrm{HD}_{2}^{\mathrm{box}} \approx
		\frac{\gamma+1}{4}\, 
		\frac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}}{\Vb}\Big(1-\frac{\Qts^2}{\Qtc^2}\Big)\ },
	\label{eq:boxHD}
\end{equation}
driver-independent at fixed output: a \emph{box} criterion, $\propto1/\Vb$,
mitigated by larger $\Vb$ and stuffing ($\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\to1$).

Two further mechanisms are real but not independently computable from
datasheets: reluctance/flux modulation ($\propto i^2\,\dd\Le/\dd x$,
growing toward the band top; proxy: shorting rings, low $\Le$, high $f_L$)
and thermal AM ($\Delta L\approx-0.034\,\xi_P\Delta T_{\mathrm{rated}}$\,dB,
$\xi_P=P_{\mathrm{dem}}/\Pmax$, slow and program-dependent, worse if one
coil serves both regimes); the same coil heating shifts $\Re$ and hence
$\Qtc,F_c$ (\cref{sec:freq}), a slow drift outside the strict LTI premise
(A1). 
Suspension creep/hysteresis is real, non-correctable, and absent from datasheets 
--- a quality prior, not a computable criterion.

\begin{proposition}[Distortion-referenced selection $=$ headroom maximization]
	\label{prop:equivdist}
	At fixed band, room, and target, every non-correctable component above is
	monotone decreasing in the headroom vector 
	$h=(\Vd,\ \Sd,\ \Vb,\ \eta_0, \ \Pmax)$. 
	Sorting candidates by predicted non-correctable distortion is
	therefore the Pareto/lexicographic order on $h$: three independent
	mechanisms (displacement, Doppler, box spring), one target, the same
	headroom ranking, now with $\Sd$ and $\Vb$ entering separately from $\Vd$.
\end{proposition}

Distortion tolerance is itself frequency-dependent: psychoacoustic masking
of harmonics by their own fundamental is strongest at low frequencies,
where critical bands are widest and absolute sensitivity is lowest
\cite{fielder2019}. 

This connects directly to the worked example: the non-correctable distortion 
total (\cref{fig:distortion-sub}) is highest in the flat, $9$--$9.5\%$ 
plateau at and below $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ 
--- precisely the low-frequency range where \cite{fielder2019}'s masking 
argument offers the most cover. Distortion, not the bare SPL ceiling, 
is therefore the leading criterion specifically in the sub-bass band, 
and it is worst exactly where it is least audible as such.
