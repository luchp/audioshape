\section{Transient demand}
\label{sec:time}

Bass programme material contains short bursts whose peak displacement
exceeds the steady-sine value at the same peak pressure. Free-mass
arguments give constant factors ($C=2$ for a cosine-phase single cycle,
$C=2\pi$ for a sine-phase single cycle) only in the limit
$\omega\gg\wc$. Both roles here operate partly near their own $F_c$, where
that limit does not apply, so no constant burst factor is assumed.

\subsection{Numerical burst factor}
\label{sec:time-ctr}

Let $e(t)=w(t)\sin(\omega t+\phi)$ be a burst of $n$ cycles with window
$w$ (rectangular or Hann) and start phase $\phi$, and let $x(t)$ solve
\cref{eq:ode} from rest. Define the transient displacement factor

\begin{equation}
	\boxed{\ C_{\mathrm{tr}}\!\left(\frac{f}{F_c},\Qtc,w,n,\phi\right)
	= \frac{\omega^{2}\max_t|x(t)|}
	       {\max_{t\in\mathcal A}|\ddot x(t)|}\ }
	\label{eq:ctr}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal A$ is the active window of the burst. The numerator is the
peak displacement including ring-down; the denominator is the peak
acceleration during the burst, which sets the radiated peak pressure
through \cref{eq:pofx}. Thus $C_{\mathrm{tr}}=1$ for steady sine, and
$C_{\mathrm{tr}}>1$ measures the extra excursion a burst costs at the same
peak output.

\Cref{eq:ctr} is evaluated by numerical solution of \cref{eq:ode} over a
declared waveform family---here one cycle, rectangular window, eight start
phases uniformly covering one period, which includes both sine and cosine
starts---and the worst phase is retained. The same solution supplies the
rms voltage, rms current, and coil power over the active window, so the
burst case is checked against \cref{eq:ampgates} with the same
per-driver limits and against the declared burst-power limit. Because
the burst response is that of the same linear system, the result is exact
within the model of \cref{sec:model} up to discretization; the waveform
family, not the physics, is the declared approximation.
\Cref{fig:transient} shows the resulting factor over the range of $f/F_c$
in which both roles operate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/fig_transient_factor}
	\caption{Numerical transient displacement factor $C_{\mathrm{tr}}$
	\cref{eq:ctr} versus $f/F_c$, worst start phase retained, for one-cycle
	rectangular and Hann-windowed bursts and $\Qtc=0.50$, $0.55$, $0.61$.
	The worked example's operating points are marked. The free-mass

	\label{fig:transient}
\end{figure}

The transient ceiling on basis B1 follows from \cref{eq:SPLL} as
$\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L,burst}}(f)=\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L}}(f)
-20\log_{10}C_{\mathrm{tr}}$, and the transient excursion utilization is
\begin{equation}
	\xi_x^{\mathrm{tr}}
	= \frac{\max_t|x(t)|}{\Xmax}
	= C_{\mathrm{tr}}\,\frac{X_{\mathrm{dem}}}{\Xmax} ,
	\label{eq:xitr}
\end{equation}

evaluated at the couch-area target. Both $\xi_x\le1$ and
$\xi_x^{\mathrm{tr}}\le1$ are hard gates; the transient one is usually the
binding excursion gate near the lower band edge of each role.

\subsection{Settling and the alignment ceiling}
\label{sec:time-settling}

The envelope decay rate $\sigma/2=\pi F_s/\Qts$ is a driver constant
(\cref{eq:alignment}), so the enclosure selects only the damped frequency
and the overshoot pattern. Step-response overshoot
$M_p=\exp(-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2})$ with $\zeta=1/(2\Qtc)$ gives
$M_p\le1\%$ for $\Qtc\le0.61$ and $M_p=0.11\%$ at the ceiling
$\Qtc\le0.55$ used here. This is an alignment diagnostic and a declared
design ceiling, not a perceptual transient-quality metric: audibility of
low-frequency phase and group-delay error is bounded by separate evidence
\cite{blauert1978}, and the ring-down of a sealed alignment is a linear,
minimum-phase effect that finite-band correction can address at the drive
cost of \cref{sec:freq-gates}.
