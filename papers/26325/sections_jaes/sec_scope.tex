\section{Scope, conventions, assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

SI units throughout. SPLs are dB values computed from the rms sound
pressure relative to $p_0=\SI{20}{\micro\pascal}$, on axis, at
$r=\SI{1}{\meter}$, half-space radiation; 
$\rho_0=\SI{1.204}{\kilogram\per\cubic\meter}$,
$c=\SI{343}{\meter\per\second}$. 
This driver-intrinsic $r=\SI{1}{\meter}$ is kept distinct throughout 
from the room's own listening distance $r_{\mathrm{listen}}$, 
introduced only from \cref{sec:roomclose} on; the two are 
never interchangeable.

Key assumptions (A1--A11; full derivations in the companion preprint
\cite{audioshape-preprint}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[A1.] Lumped-parameter LTI model; linear operation $\equiv|x|\le\Xmax$,
with all nonlinear mechanisms ($\Bl(x)$, $\Cms(x)$, flux modulation) folded
into that single limit. 
Breakup is a $ka\gtrsim1$ phenomenon well above the
bass band addressed here, so a rigid-piston model is the same premise every
T/S parameter set already presumes, not an extra restriction.

\item[A2.] Coil inductance neglected for $f\ll f_L\equiv\Re/(2\pi\Le)$;
$f_L$ is retained as an explicit validity/selection criterion rather than
simply dropped.

\item[A3.] Baffled rigid piston, far field, $ka\lesssim1$
($a=\sqrt{\Sd/\pi}$) -- the standard monopole approximation underlying
every SPL formula used below.

\item[A4.] Lossless sealed box ($Q_L\to\infty$), adiabatic air spring; box
losses are a second-order correction, not a mechanism the ranking below
depends on.

\item[A5.] $\Pmax$ is the maximum \emph{continuous} coil dissipation
$\langle i^2\rangle\Re$.
AES2/IEC~62458 datasheet ratings \cite{aes2}
approximate this within about a factor of $2$, so comparisons are
meaningful only within one rating standard; short-term (burst) dissipation
capability is $\kappa\Pmax$, $\kappa\in[2,4]$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:time}).

\item[A6.] The amplifier is unconstrained: whatever voltage/current the
driver's own limits allow is assumed available, isolating the driver's
ceiling from a separate, system-level one. 
Where amplifier headroom is itself the binding constraint, \cref{app:vented}'s 
vented alternative remains legitimate;
 App.~\ref{app:example}'s worked example also reports
the actual power/voltage the derived demand curve requires, which stays
modest in practice.

\item[A7.] $\Xmax$ is distortion-defined at $10\%$ equivalent input
distortion, per IEC~62458/Klippel \cite{iec62458,klippel2006} ($\Bl(x)$
fallen to $\approx82\%$, $\Cms(x)$ to $\approx75\%$, or $\Le(x)$ swing
$17\%$). Geometric $\Xmax$ values must be converted to this basis, or
driver comparisons are void.

\item[A8.] Room effects excluded from the free-field ceilings of
Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:distortion}; the pressure-zone bound is
reintroduced separately and quantitatively in Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}.

\item[A9.] For $N$ identical, common-signal drivers in a compact array
(spacing $\ll$ wavelength, consistent with A3), far-field volume
velocities sum linearly ($\Vd\to N\Vd$) while radiated power sums as
$N^2$ -- the standard coherent-array mutual-coupling gain, not the
$+10\log_{10}N$ of incoherent sources. A fixed acoustic target therefore
needs only $1/N^2$ of the single-driver-equivalent power from each unit.

\item[A10.] Distortion analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}) is
leading-order in $x/\Xmax$: a ranking law and order-of-magnitude
predictor, not a $\pm1$\,dB THD prediction.

\item[A11.] Targets are peak-equivalent levels at the fixed listening
position. Below $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ the room's own transfer function is flat
(Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}), so the pressure-zone relation -- not the
free-field formula evaluated at $r_{\mathrm{listen}}$ -- is the robust,
room-independent demand basis there.
\end{enumerate}
