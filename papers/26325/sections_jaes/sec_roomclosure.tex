\section{Room-specific demand}
\label{sec:room}

Everything so far is a driver property. The demand side is a room property,
and it dominates the low-bass sizing.

\subsection{Demand from a declared room transfer}
\label{sec:room-demand}

In general the required displaced volume follows from the target pressure
and a declared room transfer $H_{\mathrm{room}}$ from source displaced
volume to pressure over the primary listening area,

\begin{equation}
	V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f)=\frac{\sqrt2\,p_t}{|H_{\mathrm{room}}(f)|} ,
	\label{eq:demand-general}
\end{equation}

with $p_t$ the rms target pressure and the $\sqrt2$ converting to the peak
convention of \cref{sec:scope-bases}. Three inputs are admissible: a
measured transfer over the listening area reduced by a declared rule (centre,
spatial average, or worst case); a parameterized room-gain model with
declared slope and leakage corner; or the analytical limiting model below.
The worked example of this report uses the analytical model and reports
sensitivity to its parameters.

\subsection{Leaky pressure-zone model}
\label{sec:room-pz}

Below the first axial mode the room behaves as an acoustic compliance
shunted by leakage \cite{beranek2012}. With source volume velocity
$U=\Sd\dot x$ this gives, for the pressure-zone branch,

\begin{equation}
	|H_{\mathrm{pz}}(f)|=\frac{\rho_0c^2}{V_{\mathrm{room}}}
		\Big[1+(f_\ell/f)^2\Big]^{-1/2},
	\qquad
	f_{\mathrm{pz}}\simeq\frac{c}{2L_{\max}} ,
	\label{eq:pzdyn}
\end{equation}

a first-order high-pass relation from displacement to pressure with corner
$f_\ell$ set by construction, commonly $10$--$\SI{20}{\hertz}$. Above
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ the half-space radiation branch
$|H_{\mathrm{rad}}(f)|=\rho_0\omega^2/(2\pi r_{\mathrm{listen}})$ applies,
so the demand curve rises at $12$\,dB/oct going down, flattens near
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}$, and rises again at $6$\,dB/oct below $f_\ell$:

\begin{equation}
	V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f)=
	\begin{cases}
		\dfrac{\sqrt2\,p_t\,2\pi r_{\mathrm{listen}}}{\rho_0\omega^2},
			& f\ge f_{\mathrm{pz}},\\[2ex]
		\max\big\{V_{\mathrm{rad}},\ V_{\mathrm{pz}}(f)\big\},
			& f<f_{\mathrm{pz}},
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:Vdem}
\end{equation}

where $V_{\mathrm{rad}}=V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f_{\mathrm{pz}})$ is the
radiation branch frozen at the pressure-zone corner and
$V_{\mathrm{pz}}(f)=\sqrt2\,p_tV_{\mathrm{room}}
(\rho_0c^2)^{-1}\sqrt{1+f_\ell^2/f^2}$ is the leaky compliance branch.

This is a deliberately low-order model of a prepared room over one band and
one small area. It is \emph{not} a claim that the complete room is a
low-order system: a room remains distributed and multimodal, and above
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ individual modes are better treated as separate damped
second-order sections fitted over the listening area, or replaced by a
measured transfer \cite{toole2006,makivirta2003}. The value of the prepared
architecture of \cref{sec:intro-arch}---soffit mounting, a compact source
manifold, deliberate damping and leakage control---is that such a low-order
description is defensible over the design band, not that it is universal.

\subsection{Corner placement}
\label{sec:room-corner}

Combining \cref{eq:Vdem} with the alignment line \cref{eq:alignment} gives
the corner-placement target

\begin{equation}
	F_c\simeq f_{\mathrm{pz}}
	\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
	\frac{F_s}{\Qts}\simeq\frac{f_{\mathrm{pz}}}{\Qtc^\ast} ,
	\label{eq:Fsrule}
\end{equation}

at which the sealed roll-off and the room's flat pressure-zone branch
approximately complement one another. Two caveats apply. First, this is an
idealization: real designs require measured verification and correction,
because the complementarity holds only to the accuracy of
\cref{eq:pzdyn}. Second, the preferred $\Qtc^\ast=0.55$ and corner target may require more
than the declared per-driver cap, canonically $4\Vas$. In that case the cap
is used and the realized
$\Qtc=\Qts\sqrt{1+\Vas/\Vb}$ and
$F_c=F_s\Qtc/\Qts$ are retained; at exactly $4\Vas$,
$\Qtc=\sqrt{5/4}\,\Qts\simeq1.118\Qts$. A corner below
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ costs enclosure volume and leaves a shelf that a
correction \emph{cut} removes; a corner above $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ leaves a
sag whose correction \emph{boost} is charged to \cref{eq:Preq}, excursion,
and the burst calculation. Alignment mismatch is therefore evaluated, not
used as a geometric rejection.
