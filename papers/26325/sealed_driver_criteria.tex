\section*{Abstract}
%=============================================================================================================================
We derive, from the lumped electromechanical model of a sealed direct-radiator loudspeaker, the maximum acoustic output as a function of frequency subject to the two intrinsic driver limits: linear excursion and continuous coil dissipation. The amplifier is assumed unconstrained. Both a frequency-domain (steady sine) and a time-domain (burst/attack) analysis are carried out; they are shown to yield the \emph{same} two figures of merit, differing only by computable shape constants. The complete driver characterization for the sealed-box low-frequency problem reduces to four datasheet-computable invariants: the displacement volume set by cone area and excursion limit, the acoustic power set by reference efficiency and thermal power limit, a corner-placement parameter set by the resonance frequency and total quality factor, and an upper-validity frequency set by voice-coil resistance and inductance -- plus a regime-boundary frequency that follows from the first two. We review the widely used efficiency-bandwidth-product heuristic and show it is a legitimate enclosure-\emph{type} screen but not an absolute performance ranking; the four invariants above supply that ranking directly. A motor-materials bound proves that the displacement figure of merit and the corner-rate parameter cannot be simultaneously large, which is the rigorous form of the statement ``the sub and the attack driver cannot be the same driver.'' We further show that every distortion mechanism that no linear time-invariant filter can correct scales monotonically with the same headroom ratios, so \emph{distortion-referenced} selection reduces to headroom maximization anchored at the IEC-62458 definition of maximum linear excursion, plus two new geometric criteria: Doppler frequency modulation inversely proportional to cone area, and box-spring distortion proportional to the ratio of demanded displacement to enclosure volume. We also close the low end of the room-coupled regime: below the room pressure zone the excursion demand flattens, giving an explicit sizing rule for the displacement volume and a corresponding corner-frequency target. Finally, we extend the same lumped model by one port and prove, rather than assume, why this paper restricts itself to sealed enclosures: a vented alignment's low-frequency pressure asymptote is a provably steeper roll-off than sealed, its cone excursion is provably \emph{not} bounded by the box at all below tuning (unlike sealed, where the box caps it), and the port air velocity needed to reach the same in-room target routinely exceeds the empirical turbulence-onset threshold by an order of magnitude at realistic port sizes -- three independent, first-principles reasons the two-regime, two-driver, sealed criteria derived here are the object of this paper. A complete, unit-tested reference implementation is open-sourced at \url{https://github.com/luchp/audioshape}; every figure and table in this paper is regenerated from it.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} loudspeaker design; sealed enclosure; excursion limit; power compression; distortion; vented enclosure; driver selection

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection*{Problem statement}

The problem addressed is deliberately narrow. Given a room, a single fixed
listening position in it (``the couch''), a target sound-pressure level, a
database of driver datasheet parameters, and a DSP crossover/equalizer of
unlimited complexity between amplifier and driver: select the bass
driver(s) and sealed enclosure(s) that reproduce that target with the least
\emph{non-correctable} distortion. ``Monitor'' is used in this precise sense
throughout -- one listener, one seat, one target curve, corrected in the
digital domain wherever an LTI filter can do so (made precise in
Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}) -- not a general-purpose loudspeaker for arbitrary
placement, multiple seats, or a live-sound audience. All drivers considered
radiate into half space from a common baffle plane (a soffit wall,
Sec.~\ref{sec:assumptions}). Stereo reproduction is the default listening
scenario, but because the two channels are not a coherent point source at a
single ear, every quantity below sizes a \emph{single} channel/driver's own
output and distortion at the couch, never a hypothetical mono sum
(App.~\ref{sec:tool-scope}).

Two physical facts, both invisible to a free-field-only treatment, decide the
rest of the argument.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Room gain.} At wavelengths long compared with the room,
the air in front of the driver stops radiating and starts behaving as a lossy
pneumatic spring: below a corner frequency $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ set by the
room's largest dimension, sound pressure at the listening position tracks
cone \emph{displacement} directly, rather than its second time derivative
(Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}). A sealed system whose own corner coincides with
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ therefore tapers off below that corner \emph{for free},
letting the room supply the last octave rather than a bigger box, a port, or
more electrical power.
\item[(ii)] \textbf{Two regimes, two different jobs.} Near its own resonance
$F_s$, a sealed driver's ceiling is set by how far its cone can move before
distortion-defined $\Xmax$ is reached: displaced volume $\Vd=\Sd\Xmax$ is what
matters, and the economical driver is large, compliant, and long-throw. An
octave or more above $F_s$, the same driver's excursion at fixed acoustic
output falls as $1/f^2$, and the driver is instead limited by how much
electrical power its coil can dissipate continuously: efficiency-referenced
power $\eta_0\Pmax$ is what matters there, and the economical driver is
light, stiff, and strongly motored -- the opposite recipe.
Section~\ref{sec:invariants} proves (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}) that no single
voice-coil motor can make both quantities large at once: the two regimes want
physically different drivers, not just different alignments of the same
driver.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent These two facts jointly motivate the two design choices this
document argues for, rather than assumes: (1) a two-way bass system -- one
large, long-throw driver (or driver pair/manifold) for the excursion-limited
band near $F_s$, one smaller, strongly motored driver for the
dissipation-limited band above it -- and (2) a \emph{sealed} enclosure for
both, so the room-gain mechanism of (i) is available to every band and the
excursion ceiling stays bounded at every frequency down to DC
(Appendix~\ref{sec:vented} shows a vented alignment forfeits both
properties). Sections~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:roomclose} derive this from
the driver's own electromechanical model, Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure} turns the
result into an ordered, datasheet-only selection procedure, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion} restates the verdict compactly.

\subsection*{Roadmap}

Sec.~\ref{sec:prevwork} reviews Thiele/Small alignment theory and the $EBP$
heuristic built on top of it, and states precisely what it gets right (a
first-pass enclosure-type screen) and where it falls short (no absolute
performance ranking) -- the gap the rest of the paper closes.
Sec.~\ref{sec:assumptions} fixes SI units, the datasheet-input table, and the
numbered assumptions (A1--A11) referenced throughout. Sec.~\ref{sec:model}
writes the single second-order ODE that everything else is read off from,
and isolates the one box-invariant rate $\sigma$ that drives the rest of the
argument. Sec.~\ref{sec:freq} solves it in steady sine to get the two output
ceilings (displacement- and dissipation-limited) and the frequency $f_x$
where one hands off to the other. Sec.~\ref{sec:time} redoes the same solve
for transient (attack/burst) drive and shows the frequency-domain result was
not an artifact of the sine assumption -- only the constants move.
Sec.~\ref{sec:invariants} collects the resulting datasheet-computable
invariants and proves, via the motor materials bound (Thm.~\ref{thm:bound}),
that no single motor can make both FOMs large at once -- the rigorous form
of fact (ii) above. Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion} reframes all of this in terms
of \emph{non-correctable} distortion rather than raw SPL, which is the
criterion actually wanted and turns out to be equivalent to headroom
maximization. Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose} brings in the room: below the
pressure zone $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ the excursion demand stops rising, which
sets both a sizing rule and an $F_s$ criterion, and lets a sealed system
taper off below its corner for free -- the rigorous form of fact (i) above.
Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure} assembles all of the above into a single ordered
selection procedure, worked through concretely in Sec.~\ref{sec:example}.
Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion} closes with the design verdict and its relation to
the previous work of Sec.~\ref{sec:prevwork}. Appendix~\ref{sec:vented}
extends the same model by one port to prove, rather than assume, why this
document restricts itself to sealed enclosures; Appendix~\ref{sec:limits}
records model limitations and the reference implementation's scope; and
Appendices~\ref{app:room}--\ref{app:formulas} collect a supporting
room-pressure derivation and a formula sheet, none of which gate the main
argument.

\section{Previous work}
\label{sec:prevwork}

\noindent Thiele/Small small-signal parameters and the sealed-box alignment
line \eqref{eq:alignment} \cite{small1972} are the common starting point for
every driver/box choice below; nothing in this section revisits that theory.
What is reviewed here is the $EBP$ heuristic commonly built on top of it for
enclosure-type screening, and a few related shorthands worth correcting --
because the gap they leave open, an absolute datasheet-computable
performance ranking, is what Sec.~\ref{sec:freq} onward fills.

\subsection*{$EBP$ as an enclosure-type heuristic}

The efficiency-bandwidth product $EBP\equiv F_s/\Qes$ is a widely used single
number for a first-pass sealed-vs-vented screening: $EBP\lesssim50$ suggests
sealed, $EBP\gtrsim100$ suggests vented, the interval between either
\cite{dickason2006}. As Sec.~\ref{sec:materials} shows, $EBP$ is a genuine
motor/mass ratio, $EBP=\dfrac{B^2\sigma_c}{2\pi\rho_c}\cdot\dfrac{m_c}{\Mms}\cdot
u^{-2}$ \eqref{eq:sebound} (conductor material, conductor mass over moving
mass, overhang factor) -- a legitimate, physically grounded screening tool for
enclosure \emph{type}. It says nothing, however, about \emph{how much} SPL or
how little distortion either enclosure choice delivers once the type is
chosen: in this document $EBP$ enters only through the regime-boundary
frequency $f_x$ \eqref{eq:fx} and the corner rate \eqref{eq:cornerrate}, never
as a standalone performance ranking.

\subsection*{Three points about $\Qtc$}

\noindent Three points about $\Qtc$ bear directly on the sections that
follow:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Overshoot.} Values of order $1$--$2\%$ are often quoted for
a $\Qtc=0.55$ (Bessel-like) alignment; the exact step response gives
$M_p(0.55)=0.11\%$ \eqref{eq:Mp}, and $M_p\le1\%$ is in fact already
satisfied up to $\Qtc\le0.61$.
\item \textbf{Excursion vs.\ frequency.} $X\propto1/f^2$ only above the
sealed corner $F_c$. Below $F_c$, excursion at fixed drive
\emph{saturates} at the compliance limit \eqref{eq:PL}; the driver reaches
$\Xmax$ at any $f<F_c$ only if the required drive $P_{\mathrm{L}}(0)$ exceeds
$\Pmax$ \eqref{eq:PL0} -- a drive limit, not an excursion one.
\item \textbf{SPL ceiling vs.\ $\Qtc$.} The excursion-limited ceiling
\eqref{eq:SPLL} does not depend on $\Qtc$ at all (Cor.~\ref{cor:onlyVd}).
$\Qtc$ governs corner placement and settling (Sec.~\ref{sec:time}, step 3
of Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure}), not how loud the driver can ultimately play.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{The gap}

Neither $EBP$'s enclosure-type screen nor the assorted shorthands above
produces a single, datasheet-computable ranking that (a) is derived rather
than guessed, (b) is referenced to \emph{non-correctable} distortion rather
than raw SPL, and (c) accounts for the room gain that determines how much
low-frequency output is even needed from the driver in the first place.
Supplying that ranking, from first principles and in a form a spreadsheet
can evaluate, is the subject of Secs.~\ref{sec:assumptions}--\ref{sec:example}.

\section{Scope, conventions, assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

All quantities SI unless noted. Sound pressure levels are dB values
computed from the rms sound pressure relative to $p_0=20\,\mu\mathrm{Pa}$,
on axis, at $r=1\,$m, radiation into half space
($2\pi$~sr). Constants: $\rho_0=1.204\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $c=343\,$m/s
(so $\rho_0 c^2=1.416\times10^{5}\,$Pa).

\noindent\emph{Two distances, kept distinct throughout.} $r=1\,$m is a fixed,
driver-\emph{intrinsic} reference: every SPL ceiling in
Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:distortion} (displacement/dissipation ceilings,
distortion-vs-output curves, the worked-example table) is quoted at $r=1\,$m
purely so that two drivers can be compared on neutral ground, independent of
any particular room or seat. From Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose} on, a second,
unrelated distance $r_{\mathrm{listen}}$ appears: the actual distance from
baffle to the one fixed listening position the system is being designed for
(A11, A-list item (b) below). The two are not interchangeable and are never
equal by construction in the worked example ($r=1\,$m vs.\
$r_{\mathrm{listen}}=3\,$m, Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}): mixing them (e.g.\
plotting a $r=1\,$m ceiling against a $r_{\mathrm{listen}}$ demand curve)
silently changes the answer by $20\log_{10}r_{\mathrm{listen}}$ and is a
common bookkeeping error this document avoids by never using a bare ``$r$''
past this point without saying which one.

\medskip
\noindent Datasheet inputs used below:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
$F_s$ & free-air resonance [Hz] & $\Mms$ & moving mass incl.\ air load ($M_{\mathrm{ms}}$) [kg]\\
$\Qes,\ \Qms,\ \Qts$ & free-air quality factors & $\Re$ & DC voice-coil resistance [$\Omega$]\\
$\Vas$ & equivalent compliance volume [m$^3$] & $\Bl$ & force factor [T\,m]\\
$\Sd$ & effective piston area [m$^2$] & $\Le$ & voice-coil inductance [H]\\
$\Xmax$ & linear excursion, peak one-way [m] & $\Pmax$ & rated continuous power [W]\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Assumptions (referenced as A1--A9):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[A1.] Lumped-parameter LTI model; ``linear operation'' $\equiv |x|\le\Xmax$.
      All nonlinear mechanisms ($\Bl(x)$, $\Cms(x)$, flux modulation) are absorbed
      into the single number $\Xmax$.
\item[A2.] Voice-coil inductance neglected: valid for $f\ll f_L\equiv\Re/(2\pi\Le)$.
      $f_L$ is retained as an explicit validity/selection criterion
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:invariants}).
\item[A3.] Baffled rigid piston, far field, $ka\lesssim1$
      ($a=\sqrt{\Sd/\pi}$): $p(r,t)=\dfrac{\rho_0\Sd\,\ddot{x}(t-r/c)}{2\pi r}$.
\item[A4.] Lossless sealed box ($Q_L\to\infty$), adiabatic air spring.
\item[A5.] $\Pmax$ is interpreted as the maximum \emph{continuous coil dissipation}
      $\langle i^2\rangle \Re$. Datasheet AES/IEC ratings \cite{aes2} approximate this
      within a factor $\sim$2; comparisons are meaningful only within one rating
      standard. Short-term (burst) dissipation capability is $\kappa\Pmax$ with
      $\kappa\in[2,4]$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:time}).
\item[A6.] The amplifier is unconstrained: any voltage/current the driver limits
      allow is available. (User premise: home system, driver limits bind first.)
\item[A7.] $\Xmax$ peak one-way, \emph{distortion-defined} per IEC 62458 /
      Klippel \cite{iec62458,klippel2006}:
      the displacement at which equivalent input distortion reaches $10\%$
      ($\Bl(x)$ fallen to $\approx82\%$, or $\Cms(x)$ to $\approx75\%$, or
      $\Le(x)$ swing $17\%$). Geometric $\Xmax$ values must be converted or the
      comparison is void; Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion} depends on this anchoring.
\item[A8.] Room effects excluded from all ceilings; the pressure-zone bound is
      quantified separately in Appendix~\ref{app:room}.
\item[A9.] Single driver by default. For $N$ identical, common-signal drivers in a
      compact array (unit spacing $\ll$ wavelength, consistent with A3's
      $ka\lesssim1$): volume velocities sum \emph{linearly} in the far field,
      $\Vd\to N\Vd$ (already the scaling used in \eqref{eq:SPLL}). The same linear
      summation gives $N^2$, not $N$, for power: driving every unit at its own
      $\Pmax$ radiates $N^2\eta_0\Pmax$ ($+20\log_{10}N$\,dB per doubling, the
      standard coherent sub-array/mutual-coupling gain --- not the $+10\log_{10}N$
      of $N$ incoherent sources); conversely, a \emph{fixed} acoustic target needs
      only $1/N^2$ of the single-driver-equivalent electrical power from each unit
      (used in Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}'s thermal term). Only the change this
      induces in each unit's own radiation loading ($F_s$, $Q_{ts}$ shift with
      proximity) is neglected as second order.
\item[A10.] Distortion analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}) works to leading Taylor
      order in $x/\Xmax$; frequency-dependent transfer weightings of the distortion
      products, which are $O(1)$ in band, are suppressed. Results are ranking laws
      and order-of-magnitude predictors, not $\pm1$\,dB THD predictions.
\item[A11.] Targets are peak-equivalent levels at the listening position; near and
      below $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ the robust demand basis is the pressure-zone relation
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}), since the free-field formula with $r=$ listening
      distance under- or over-estimates by the modal gain in $[f_{\mathrm{pz}},
      \,{\sim}4f_{\mathrm{pz}}]$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model and exact transfer relations}
\label{sec:model}

Everything below is read off one lumped-parameter ODE, so it is written down once,
exactly, before any regime-specific approximation is made.

Terminal voltage $e(t)$, current $i(t)$, cone displacement $x(t)$ (outward positive).
Electrical side (A2): $e=\Re i+\Bl\dot{x}$. Force on cone: $F=\Bl\, i$.
Mechanics with total stiffness $k_t$ (suspension $+$ box):
\begin{equation}
\Mms\ddot{x}+\Rms\dot{x}+k_t x=\Bl\, i ,\qquad
k_t=\frac{1}{\Cms}+\frac{\rho_0c^2\Sd^2}{\Vb}
=\frac{1}{\Cms}\,(1+\alpha),\qquad
\alpha\equiv\frac{\Vas}{\Vb},
\label{eq:newton}
\end{equation}
using $\Vas\equiv\rho_0c^2\Cms\Sd^2$. Eliminating $i$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \ddot{x}+\sigma\dot{x}+\wc^2 x=\frac{\Bl}{\Re \Mms}\,e(t)\ }
\qquad
\sigma\equiv\sm+\se,\quad
\sm\equiv\frac{\Rms}{\Mms},\quad
\se\equiv\frac{\Bl^2}{\Re \Mms},\quad
\wc^2\equiv\frac{k_t}{\Mms}.
\label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
Free-air values: $\ws=2\pi F_s=\sqrt{1/(\Mms\Cms)}$, and by definition
\begin{equation}
\Qes=\frac{\ws}{\se},\qquad \Qms=\frac{\ws}{\sm},\qquad \Qts=\frac{\ws}{\sigma},
\qquad EBP\equiv\frac{F_s}{\Qes}=\frac{\se}{2\pi}.
\label{eq:Qdefs}
\end{equation}
In-box values: $\wc=\ws\sqrt{1+\alpha}$, $\Qtc=\wc/\sigma$, $\Qmc=\wc/\sm$.

\begin{prop}[Alignment line; box invariance of the damping rate --
after \cite{small1972}]
\label{prop:invariance}
The damping rate $\sigma$ contains no stiffness and is therefore independent of the
enclosure. Hence for every sealed alignment of a given driver
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \frac{\wc}{\Qtc}=\frac{\ws}{\Qts}=\sigma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_c = \Qtc\,\frac{F_s}{\Qts}\ },
\qquad
\Vb=\frac{\Vas}{(\Qtc/\Qts)^2-1},\qquad \Qtc>\Qts .
\label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
$\sigma=(\Rms+\Bl^2/\Re)/\Mms$; the box adds only $k_t$. Then
$\wc/\Qtc=\sigma=\ws/\Qts$ and
$F_c/F_s=\sqrt{1+\alpha}=\Qtc/\Qts$, giving $\alpha=(\Qtc/\Qts)^2-1$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Consequences: (i) a sealed driver owns exactly \emph{one} free parameter,
the position along the line $\wc=\Qtc\sigma$; (ii) choosing the damping $\Qtc$
\emph{fixes} the corner: $F_c=\Qtc\,(F_s/\Qts)$. The driver constant
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}=\frac{F_s}{\Qts}=EBP\left(1+\frac{\Qes}{\Qms}\right)\approx EBP
\label{eq:cornerrate}
\end{equation}
will be called the \emph{corner rate}. This is the rigorous content of $EBP$: it does
not measure output capability; it measures where the corner lands for a chosen
$\Qtc$ (and, Sec.~\ref{sec:time}, how fast transients settle).

Steady sine at $\omega$, phasor amplitudes ($\hat{x}$ peak):
\begin{align}
\hat{x}(\omega)&=\frac{\Bl}{\Re\Mms}\,
\frac{\hat{e}}{\sqrt{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sigma^2\omega^2}},
\label{eq:xofe}\\
\hat{\imath}(\omega)&=\frac{\Mms}{\Bl}\,
\hat{x}\,\sqrt{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2}
\qquad\text{(from \eqref{eq:newton}: only \emph{mechanical} damping enters)},
\label{eq:iofx}\\
\hat{p}(\omega)&=\frac{\rho_0\Sd\,\omega^2\hat{x}}{2\pi r}\qquad\text{(A3)}.
\label{eq:pofx}
\end{align}
Equation \eqref{eq:iofx} is Newton's law read backwards: whatever the source does,
the current must supply cone inertia, mechanical loss, and spring force. The
electrical damping $\se$ appears in \eqref{eq:xofe} (voltage drive) but never in
\eqref{eq:iofx}.

\section{Frequency domain: the two ceilings}
\label{sec:freq}

Solve \eqref{eq:ode} in steady sine and ask, at each frequency, which of the two
driver limits ($\Xmax$ or $\Pmax$) is reached first under an unconstrained
amplifier (A6) -- the answer is one ceiling per limit, and a crossover frequency
between them.

\subsection{Displacement ceiling}

\begin{prop}[Excursion-limited SPL]
\label{prop:xceiling}
With A6, the maximum sine pressure under $|x|\le\Xmax$ is, at every frequency,
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_{\mathrm{L}}(\omega)=\frac{\rho_0\,\omega^2 \Vd}{2\pi r},
\qquad \Vd\equiv\Sd\Xmax,
\qquad
\boxed{\ \mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L}}(f)=108.5+20\log_{10}\!\big(f^2\,\Vd\big)\ \mathrm{dB}\ }
\label{eq:SPLL}
\end{equation}
($+12$\,dB/oct; constant $=20\log_{10}(\sqrt{2}\pi\rho_0/p_0)-3.01$, i.e.\ rms at
$r=1$\,m).
\end{prop}

\begin{cor}[Irrelevance of everything else]
\label{cor:onlyVd}
$\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L}}$ is independent of $\Qtc$, $F_c$, $\Bl$, $\Re$, $\Mms$,
$\Pmax$. Under A6 the alignment determines only the \emph{drive} required to reach
$\Xmax$, never the pressure produced at $\Xmax$. In the excursion regime the complete
driver ranking is the single number $\Vd$.
\end{cor}

This corrects the intuition that ``max SPL at $(f,\Qtc)$'' depends on $\Qtc$: it does
not, until a drive limit is added. The drive actually required to sit at the ceiling
is, from \eqref{eq:iofx} with $\hat{x}=\Xmax$ and $P=\tfrac12\hat{\imath}^2\Re$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\
P_{\mathrm{L}}(\omega)=\frac{\Re\Mms^2\Xmax^2}{2\,\Bl^2}
\Big[(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2\Big]
=\frac{\Mms \Qes \Xmax^2}{4\pi F_s}
\Big[(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2\Big]\ }
\label{eq:PL}
\end{equation}
a ``bathtub'': minimum near $\wc$ (at resonance only $\Rms$ must be fed), finite DC
limit
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{L}}(0)=\frac{\Mms\Qes\Xmax^2\,\wc^4}{4\pi F_s},
\label{eq:PL0}
\end{equation}
and $\propto\omega^4$ growth above $\wc$. Equation \eqref{eq:PL0} is the exact
price of the ``sealed $+$ DSP boost'' strategy: holding $\Xmax$ arbitrarily far below
the corner costs a \emph{finite, computable} power (worked example:
Sec.~\ref{sec:example}). More generally, the power to hold a flat target pressure
$p_t$ (rms) below the corner follows from
\eqref{eq:xofe}--\eqref{eq:pofx}:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{req}}(\omega)=P_{\mathrm{pb}}\cdot
\frac{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2}{\omega^4},
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{pb}}=\frac{2\pi r^2 p_t^2}{\rho_0 c\,\eta_0},
\label{eq:EQtax}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ an EQ tax of $(\wc/\omega)^4$ (12\,dB/oct in power) below the corner. FIR/DSP
correction is ``free'' in fidelity (LTI, invertible) but pays \eqref{eq:EQtax} in heat
and \eqref{eq:SPLL} in excursion. Taken at face value, \eqref{eq:EQtax} diverges as
$\omega\to0$ for a flat target; in practice the room's own pressure-zone gain
(Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}) flattens the \emph{required} SPL below $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$,
so the real demand curve --- and the power and voltage needed to sit on it ---
saturates instead. The worked example (Fig.~\ref{fig:spl-sub}) needs only
$7.8$--$316$~W and $29$--$50$~V per unit across the plotted band, comfortably
inside a common Class-D amplifier's rating: an unconstrained amplifier (A6) costs
nothing exotic here.

\subsection{Dissipation ceiling}

\begin{prop}[Thermally limited SPL]
\label{prop:thermal}
At coil dissipation $P$ (rms current $\sqrt{P/\Re}$), the achievable pressure is
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{T}}(\omega)=\frac{\rho_0\Sd \Bl}{2\pi r \Mms}\sqrt{\frac{P}{\Re}}\;
\frac{\omega^2}{\sqrt{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2}}
\ \xrightarrow[\ \omega\gg\wc\ ]{}\
p_{\mathrm{T}\infty}=\frac{\rho_0\Sd \Bl}{2\pi r \Mms}\sqrt{\frac{P}{\Re}} .
\label{eq:pT}
\end{equation}
The passband value is exactly the classical efficiency relation: with
\begin{equation}
\eta_0=\frac{\rho_0}{2\pi c}\,\frac{\Bl^2\Sd^2}{\Re\Mms^2}
=\frac{4\pi^2}{c^3}\,\frac{F_s^3\Vas}{\Qes}
=9.78\times10^{-7}\ \frac{F_s^3\,\Vas[\mathrm{m^3}]}{\Qes},
\label{eq:eta0}
\end{equation}
one has $p_{\mathrm{T}\infty}^2=\rho_0 c\,\eta_0 P/(2\pi r^2)$ and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{T}}=112.2+10\log_{10}\!\big(\eta_0\Pmax\big)\ \mathrm{dB}\ }
\label{eq:SPLT}
\end{equation}
(flat above $F_c$; $112.2=10\log_{10}\!\big(\rho_0c/(2\pi p_0^2)\big)$).
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
Insert $\hat\imath=\sqrt{2P/\Re}$ into \eqref{eq:iofx}, then \eqref{eq:pofx}; the
identity in \eqref{eq:eta0} follows from
$\Qes=\ws\Mms\Re/\Bl^2$, $\ws^2=1/(\Mms\Cms)$, $\Vas=\rho_0c^2\Cms\Sd^2$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Hence in the dissipation regime the complete ranking is the single number
$\eta_0\Pmax$ (acoustic watts), datasheet-computable via \eqref{eq:eta0}. In
mechanical terms $\eta_0\Pmax\propto\big(\Sd\Bl/(\Mms\sqrt{\Re})\big)^2\Pmax$.

\subsection{Master equation and the regime boundary}

Both limits combine into one statement:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\
p_{\max}(\omega)=\frac{\rho_0\Sd\,\omega^2}{2\pi r}\;
\min\!\Big\{\Xmax,\ \ X_{\mathrm{T}}(\omega)\Big\},\qquad
X_{\mathrm{T}}(\omega)=\frac{\Bl\sqrt{2\Pmax/\Re}}
{\Mms\sqrt{(\wc^2-\omega^2)^2+\sm^2\omega^2}} \ }
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
(peak quantities; rms $=/\sqrt2$). Near $\wc$, $X_{\mathrm{T}}$ is large (little
current needed at resonance) so excursion always binds there; far above $\wc$,
$X_{\mathrm{T}}\propto\omega^{-2}$ and dissipation binds. The crossover
($X_{\mathrm{T}}=\Xmax$, $\omega\gg\wc$), equivalently
$\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L}}(f_x)=\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{T}}$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\
f_x=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{2\,\Pmax \Bl^2}{\Re\Mms^2\Xmax^2}\right)^{1/4}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{4\pi\,EBP\;\Pmax}{\Mms\,\Xmax^{2}}\right)^{1/4}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{4\pi c\,\eta_0\Pmax}{\rho_0\,\Vd^{2}}\right)^{1/4}
\ }
\label{eq:fx}
\end{equation}
(the three forms are identical by \eqref{eq:eta0}; note where $EBP$
\emph{legitimately} appears). Interpretation:
\[
f<f_x:\ \text{ceiling}=\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L}}(f)\ \ (\text{improve }\Vd),
\qquad
f>f_x:\ \text{ceiling}=\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{T}}\ \ (\text{improve }\eta_0\Pmax).
\]

\begin{rmk}[Ceiling regime vs.\ target regime]
\label{rmk:target}
$f_x$ ranks ceilings. Whether a driver is adequate is decided at the \emph{target}
$L_t$: requirement $\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{L}}(f_1)\ge L_t$ at the band bottom $f_1$
and $\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{T}}\ge L_t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure}). A driver can be
``thermally limited above 33\,Hz'' at its ceiling yet never approach thermal limits
at a 110\,dB home target.
\end{rmk}

\section{Time domain: attack, overshoot, settling}
\label{sec:time}

Model a bass attack as a one-cycle acceleration burst of amplitude $A$ and angular
frequency $\omega\gg\wc$: $p$-peak $\hat p=\rho_0\Sd A/(2\pi r)$. Over the burst
duration the spring and damping act only at $O((\wc/\omega)^2)$, $O(\sigma/\omega)$
(free-mass limit), so $x\simeq\iint a$.

\begin{lem}[Burst shape factor]
\label{lem:shape}
Define $C\equiv \omega^2\max_t|x(t)|/A$ for a burst of peak acceleration $A$.
Then $C=1$ for steady sine, and for one-cycle bursts $a(t)=A\sin\omega t$ resp.\
$A\cos\omega t$ on $[0,2\pi/\omega]$:
\[
x_{\sin}(t)=\frac{A}{\omega}t-\frac{A}{\omega^2}\sin\omega t
\ \Rightarrow\ C_{\sin}=2\pi;
\qquad
x_{\cos}(t)=\frac{A}{\omega^2}(1-\cos\omega t)
\ \Rightarrow\ C_{\cos}=2 .
\]
For $n$ cycles in sine phase, $C=2\pi n$. Hence transient program demands
$C\in[2,\,2\pi]$ ($6$--$16$\,dB) \emph{more excursion per unit peak pressure} than
steady sine; the sine-phase penalty is the residual velocity DC
$\langle\dot x\rangle=A/\omega>0$ during the burst.
\end{lem}

Consequences (peak-equivalent level $\hat L\equiv20\log_{10}(\hat p/(\sqrt2 p_0))$,
which coincides with rms SPL for steady sine):
\begin{align}
\text{excursion ceiling:}\quad
&\hat L_{\mathrm{L,burst}}(f)=108.5+20\log_{10}(f^2\Vd)-20\log_{10}C,
\label{eq:burstL}\\
\text{force ceiling:}\quad
&\hat A\le \frac{\Bl}{\Mms}\sqrt{\frac{2\kappa\Pmax}{\Re}}
\ \Rightarrow\
\hat L_{\mathrm{T,burst}}=112.2+10\log_{10}(\eta_0\,\kappa\Pmax),
\label{eq:burstT}
\end{align}
where $\kappa$ (A5) is the short-term dissipation headroom.

\begin{prop}[Equivalence of the two analyses; boundary shift]
\label{prop:equiv}
Time-domain and frequency-domain limits produce the same two figures of merit,
$\Vd$ and $\eta_0\Pmax$, rescaled by the constants $C$ and $\kappa$. The regime
boundary for transients moves \emph{up} to
\begin{equation}
\hat f_x=f_x\,(\kappa C^2)^{1/4}
\qquad(\kappa=4,\ C=2\ \Rightarrow\ \hat f_x=2f_x).
\label{eq:fxburst}
\end{equation}
\end{prop}

\noindent So for attack-type program the excursion regime extends roughly an octave
higher than the sine analysis suggests: even a ``power-limited'' midbass band is,
for kicks, largely $\Vd$-limited. Both drivers therefore need both FOMs checked, each
at its own band edge (Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure}).

\subsection{Ring-down: overshoot, decay, spectral tail}

After the burst the state $(x_0,v_0)\neq(0,0)$ relaxes along the homogeneous
solution of \eqref{eq:ode}: $x\propto e^{-\sigma t/2}\cos(\omega_d t+\varphi)$,
$\omega_d=\wc\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$, $\zeta=1/(2\Qtc)$.

\begin{prop}[Settling is a driver constant; ring shape is a box choice]
\label{prop:decay}
The envelope decay rate of every sealed alignment of a given driver equals
$\sigma/2=\pi F_s/\Qts$ (box-invariant, Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}); the box selects
only $\omega_d$ and thus the overshoot pattern. Cycles to $-60$\,dB
$\simeq 2.2\,\Qtc$. Step-response (displacement) overshoot:
\begin{equation}
M_p=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi\zeta}{\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}\right),\qquad \zeta=\frac{1}{2\Qtc}:
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
\Qtc & 0.50 & 0.55 & 0.60 & 0.65 & 0.707 & 0.80 & 1.00\\\hline
M_p\,[\%] & 0 & 0.11 & 0.95 & 2.3 & 4.3 & 8.1 & 16.3
\end{array}
\label{eq:Mp}
\end{equation}
Thresholds: $M_p\le1\%\Leftrightarrow\Qtc\le0.61$;
$M_p\le5\%\Leftrightarrow\Qtc\le0.72$.
\end{prop}

\noindent\emph{Spurious tail pressure.} The ring radiates
$p_{\mathrm{tail}}\propto\ddot x_{\mathrm{tail}}$. Worst case (sine-phase burst,
$x_0=CA/\omega^2$, $v_0=0$):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\hat p_{\mathrm{tail}}}{\hat p_{\mathrm{burst}}}
\;\le\; C\left(\frac{\wc}{\omega}\right)^{2},
\qquad\text{best phase: } O\!\big((\wc/\omega)^{3}\big).
\label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
Transient cleanliness improves 12\,dB per octave of corner-below-band. This, not
group delay per se, is the quantitative argument for placing the sub's corner well
under the kick band, and it is LTI --- FIR can pre-compensate it exactly, at the cost
\eqref{eq:EQtax}, \eqref{eq:SPLL}. The invariants FIR cannot touch remain
$\Vd$, $\eta_0\Pmax$, $\sigma$.

\section{The driver invariants, and what $EBP$ actually is}
\label{sec:invariants}

Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:time} produced four quantities that survived both
the sine and the transient analysis unchanged; collecting them turns the whole
model into a short, datasheet-computable checklist. A sealed-box LF driver is
characterized, for selection purposes, by
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
\# & invariant & regime & datasheet formula\\
\midrule
1 & $\Vd=\Sd\Xmax$ & excursion ($f<f_x$; transients $f<\hat f_x$) & direct\\
2 & $\eta_0\Pmax$ & dissipation ($f>f_x$) & $9.78\!\times\!10^{-7}F_s^3\Vas\Pmax/\Qes$\\
3 & $\sigma/2\pi=F_s/\Qts$ & corner placement, settling rate & direct\\
4 & $f_L=\Re/(2\pi\Le)$ & upper validity of (1)--(3) & direct\\
5 & $\Sd$ (alone) & Doppler FM at fixed output, Sec.~\ref{sec:doppler} & direct\\
\midrule
-- & $f_x$, eq.~\eqref{eq:fx} & boundary between 1 and 2 & derived\\
-- & $F_c=\Qtc F_s/\Qts$, $\Vb=\dfrac{\Vas}{(\Qtc/\Qts)^2-1}$ & box, given target $\Qtc$ & derived\\
-- & $V_{\mathrm{dem}}/\Vb$, eq.~\eqref{eq:boxHD} & box-spring distortion (box-level) & derived\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{$EBP$ as coil-mass fraction; the motor materials bound}
\label{sec:materials}

A common rule of thumb states $EBP\propto V_c/\Mms$ (coil volume over moving
mass) as a motor-strength proxy (Sec.~\ref{sec:prevwork}); it is correct in
spirit. Here is the exact, dimensionally consistent version. Overhung motor:
gap height $h_g$, coil height $h_c=h_g+2\Xmax$
(geometric $\Xmax$), wire length $\ell$, cross-section $A_w$, conductivity
$\sigma_c$, material density $\rho_c$, coil conductor mass $m_c=\rho_c\ell A_w$.
Only the in-gap fraction contributes to $\Bl$; fringing extends the effective gap to
$h_g'\approx 2h_g$ (model statement, $\pm$; this is the one non-first-principles
constant here). With $u\equiv h_c/h_g'$ (effective overhang factor, $u\ge1$ for
overhung):
\begin{equation}
\Bl=B\,\ell/u,\qquad \Re=\frac{\ell}{\sigma_c A_w}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\se=\frac{\Bl^2}{\Re\Mms}=\frac{B^2\sigma_c}{\rho_c}\cdot\frac{m_c}{\Mms}\cdot\frac{1}{u^2}.
\label{eq:sebound}
\end{equation}

\begin{thm}[Motor materials bound]
\label{thm:bound}
For an overhung motor,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\
EBP\cdot u^{2}
=\frac{\se}{2\pi}\,u^2
=\frac{B^{2}\sigma_c}{2\pi\rho_c}\cdot\frac{m_c}{\Mms}
\;\le\;
\underbrace{\frac{B^{2}\sigma_c}{2\pi\rho_c}}_{K_{\mathrm{mat}}}\,\beta_{\max}\ }
\qquad \beta_{\max}=\Big(\tfrac{m_c}{\Mms}\Big)_{\max}\lesssim0.35 .
\label{eq:thmbound}
\end{equation}
Copper: $K_{\mathrm{mat}}=1059\,B^2$ Hz; aluminium: $2063\,B^2$ Hz
($\sigma_c/\rho_c=6.65\times10^{3}$ resp.\ $1.30\times10^{4}$ S\,m$^2$/kg).
\end{thm}

\noindent\emph{Numerical content} ($B=1.0$--$1.2$ T, Cu, $\beta_{\max}=0.35$: bound
$\approx 370$--$530$ Hz):
\[
\text{sub class: } u\approx3\ (\Xmax\!\approx\!2.5h_g)\Rightarrow EBP\lesssim45\,\mathrm{Hz};
\qquad
\text{attack class: } EBP\approx135\,\mathrm{Hz}\Rightarrow u\lesssim1.8\
(\Xmax\lesssim1.4\,h_g).
\]
A single driver with sub-class stroke ($u=3$) \emph{and} attack-class corner rate
(135 Hz) would need $EBP\cdot u^2\approx1200$ Hz, i.e.\ $\beta B^2\approx1.1$ ---
impossible for $\beta<1$, $B\lesssim1.4$ T. This is the theorem behind the empirical
observation that the ideal drivers of the two regimes are different physical objects.
Three independent mechanisms enforce the split:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \eqref{eq:thmbound}: stroke $u$ suppresses the corner rate $\sigma$
      quadratically at fixed coil fraction;
\item Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}: one driver has one $\sigma$, hence one
      $(F_c,\Qtc)$ line --- it cannot put a damped corner both at 23 Hz and at 80 Hz;
\item A2: stroke requires a tall coil, hence large $\Le$, hence low
      $f_L=\Re/(2\pi\Le)$ intruding into the attack band (worked example: 139 Hz for
      the 18'', 1.2 kHz for the 12'').
\end{enumerate}
Escape routes (underhung/XBL$^2$, dual gap, neo focusing, shorting rings, aluminium
coils) buy factors of $\sim\!\!2$, not the required $\sim\!5$, and trade against
$\beta$ or $B$ inside the same bound.

\section{Non-correctable distortion: scaling laws and the distortion-referenced criterion}
\label{sec:distortion}

The invariants above rank drivers by \emph{output}. But the premise of this
document is that output is not the right currency: a DSP-aligned system
(App.~\ref{sec:tool-scope}) can move gain, phase, and even group delay around
almost for free, so the only distortion that should decide a ranking is the kind
no filter can remove. This section makes that precise, then shows it reduces
back to a headroom criterion -- so nothing above is wasted, but the answer it
gives is different in general from ranking on SPL alone.

\subsection{What LTI correction can and cannot do}

Write the plant as $p = H\ast e + \mathcal{N}[e]$, with $H$ the small-signal
response \eqref{eq:xofe}--\eqref{eq:pofx} and $\mathcal{N}$ the nonlinear residual.

\begin{prop}[FIR correctability boundary]
\label{prop:LTI}
(i) In-band, $H$ is minimum-phase (the sealed-box $p/e$ transfer
$\propto s^2/(s^2+\sigma s+\wc^2)$ has all zeros/poles in the closed left half
plane), so a causal, stable LTI pre-filter $G\approx H^{-1}$ exists on any band
$[f_1,f_2]$, $f_1>0$: magnitude, phase, group delay, corner sag, and ring-down tail
\eqref{eq:tail} are all exactly correctable (at the drive costs
\eqref{eq:SPLL}, \eqref{eq:EQtax}).
(ii) No LTI $G$ reduces $\mathcal{N}$ for all program: if it did, then from
linearity of $G$ and of the corrected small-signal path, scaling the input
$e\to a e$ would require $\mathcal{N}[a\,Ge]=a\,\mathcal{N}[Ge]$ for all $a$,
i.e.\ $\mathcal{N}$ linear on the signal space --- contradiction unless
$\mathcal{N}\equiv0$.
\end{prop}

\noindent Hence the selection-relevant driver property is exactly the
signal-level-dependent part. (Nonlinear model-based pre-distortion --- Klippel
controllers, delay modulation for Doppler --- can attack $\mathcal{N}$, but that is
not an FIR filter and requires per-unit parameter identification; excluded here.)
Linear-phase (rather than minimum-phase) inversion additionally trades the tail for
pre-ring; a choice of taste, not of physics.

\subsection{Displacement-driven nonlinearity: the headroom law}

Expand motor and suspension about rest:
\begin{equation}
\Bl(x)=\Bl_0\,(1-\beta_1 x-\beta_2 x^2-\dots),\qquad
\frac{1}{\Cms(x)}=\frac{1}{C_0}\,(1+\gamma_1 x+\gamma_2 x^2+\dots).
\label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
For $x=X\cos\omega t$, the leading distortion products (transfer weights suppressed,
A10) are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HD}_2 \sim \tfrac12\,|\beta_1^{\mathrm{eff}}|\,X,\qquad
\mathrm{HD}_3 \sim \tfrac14\,|\beta_2^{\mathrm{eff}}|\,X^2,
\qquad
\text{IMD}_{2}(\text{bass}\to\text{mid}) \sim |\beta_1^{\mathrm{eff}}|\,X_1 ,
\label{eq:HDproducts}
\end{equation}
with $\beta^{\mathrm{eff}}$ the appropriate combinations of
$\beta_i,\gamma_i$ (asymmetries $\to$ even order, symmetric droop $\to$ odd order).
By A7 the distortion-defined $\Xmax$ \emph{anchors} the sum at
$D(\Xmax)\approx10\%$. Therefore, with the \textbf{utilization}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \xi_x(f)\equiv\frac{X_{\mathrm{dem}}(f)}{\Xmax}
=\frac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f)}{\Vd},\qquad
D(\xi_x)\approx d_2\,\xi_x+d_3\,\xi_x^{2},\quad d_2+d_3\approx0.1\ }
\label{eq:HDscale}
\end{equation}
and the rigorous bounds $0.1\,\xi_x^{2}\le D\le 0.1\,\xi_x$. Consequences:
\begin{itemize}
\item At fixed target and frequency, $X_{\mathrm{dem}}\propto p_t/(\Sd f^2)$, so
motor/suspension distortion is a \emph{monotone function of the headroom on the
displacement ceiling} \eqref{eq:SPLL}: sorting by this distortion class $=$ sorting
by $\Vd$. The v1 FOM survives unchanged; its meaning sharpens from ``max SPL'' to
``distortion at given SPL.''
\item A distortion budget $D^\ast$ converts to a utilization cap
$\xi^\ast=D^\ast/0.1$ (conservative, even-order-dominated driver) or
$\sqrt{D^\ast/0.1}$ (symmetric driver). E.g.\ $D^\ast=3\%$:
$\xi^\ast=0.3$--$0.55$; the $\Vd$ requirement is $1/\xi^\ast\approx2$--$3\times$
the bare SPL-ceiling requirement. \textbf{This factor, not the SPL math, sizes the
sub.}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Doppler (FM) distortion: the second use of area}
\label{sec:doppler}

A cone at instantaneous position $x_1(t)$ radiating a component at $f_2$ delivers it
with propagation phase $\varphi=\omega_2(t-(r-x_1)/c)$: phase modulation with index
$m=k_2X_1=2\pi f_2X_1/c$, i.e.\ sideband pairs at $f_2\pm f_1$ of relative level
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \frac{J_1(m)}{J_0(m)}\approx\frac{m}{2}=\frac{\pi f_2X_1}{c}
=\frac{\pi f_2}{c}\cdot\frac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f_1)}{\Sd}\ }
\label{eq:doppler}
\end{equation}
Properties, all first-principles: (i) it is FM --- maximally non-LTI-correctable;
(ii) at fixed output it scales as $1/\Sd$ and is \emph{independent of} $\Xmax$:
among equal-$\Vd$ drivers, the larger cone is strictly cleaner --- area enters
selection a second time, separately from $\Vd$; (iii) it vanishes identically under
band splitting, because $f_2$ no longer rides on the cone doing $X_1$. Equation
\eqref{eq:doppler} evaluated at the top of a candidate band \emph{is} the
quantitative split criterion: choose the split $f_{\mathrm{sp}}$ such that
$\pi f_{\mathrm{sp}}X_1^{\max}/c\le D^\ast_{\mathrm{IM}}$.

\subsection{Sealed-box air-spring distortion}

Adiabatic compression of the box gives the restoring pressure
$\Delta P=P_0[(1-\Sd x/\Vb)^{-\gamma}-1]
=\gamma P_0\frac{\Sd x}{\Vb}\big[1+\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\frac{\Sd x}{\Vb}+\dots\big]$,
an asymmetric spring. Weighting by the box share of total stiffness
$\alpha/(1+\alpha)=1-(\Qts/\Qtc)^2$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \mathrm{HD}_{2}^{\mathrm{box}}\approx\frac{\gamma+1}{4}\,
\frac{\Sd X}{\Vb}\Big(1-\frac{\Qts^2}{\Qtc^2}\Big)
=\frac{\gamma+1}{4}\,\frac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}}{\Vb}\Big(1-\frac{\Qts^2}{\Qtc^2}\Big)\ }
\label{eq:boxHD}
\end{equation}
--- driver-independent at fixed output: a \emph{box} criterion, $\propto1/\Vb$.
Stuffing helps twice: $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\to1$ lowers the prefactor and raises
the effective volume by up to $\gamma=1.4$. A sealed spring at
$V_{\mathrm{dem}}/\Vb\sim1\%$ distorts at the $0.1$--$0.3\%$ level
\eqref{eq:boxHD} (worked example, Sec.~\ref{sec:example}: $0.11\%$); the vented
port's own, considerably larger, air-velocity-driven distortion is quantified
separately in App.~\ref{sec:vented} rather than asserted here.

\subsection{Flux modulation, thermal AM, creep}
\label{sec:otherdist}

\emph{Reluctance/flux modulation.} Coil current modulates gap flux
($\Bl(i)$) and creates reluctance force $F=\tfrac12 i^2\,\dd\Le/\dd x$: distortion
$\propto i^2$, growing toward the band top where $i$ is large and $x$ small ---
the complement of the displacement mechanisms. Not computable from standard
datasheets ($\dd\Le/\dd x$, $\Le(i)$ unpublished); proxies: shorting
rings/copper cap declared, small $\Le$, high $f_L$ --- the same $f_L$ already in the
invariant list. Where Klippel scans \cite{klippel2006} are published, use them
directly.

\emph{Thermal AM.} Coil heating modulates sensitivity:
$\Delta L=-8.69\ln(1+\alpha_{\mathrm{Cu}}\Delta T)\approx-0.034\,\Delta T$ dB
($\alpha_{\mathrm{Cu}}=3.9\times10^{-3}/$K), with coil time constants of seconds:
a slow, program-dependent AM, plus \emph{cross-band} modulation if one coil serves
both regimes (bass heating compresses the attack band) --- a further independent
argument for the two-driver split. Datasheet proxy: thermal utilization
$\xi_P=P_{\mathrm{dem}}/\Pmax$; $\Delta T\approx\xi_P\,\Delta T_{\mathrm{rated}}$.

\emph{Suspension creep/hysteresis} (viscoelastic memory): real, non-correctable,
absent from datasheets; a quality prior on the suspension, not a computable
criterion. Listed for completeness.

\subsection{Taxonomy and the equivalence of distortion- and headroom-sorting}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
mechanism & scaling @ fixed target & datasheet? & LTI-corr.? & mitigation\\
\midrule
$\Bl(x),\Cms(x)$ HD/IMD & $d_2\xi_x+d_3\xi_x^2$, \eqref{eq:HDscale} & yes ($\Vd$, A7) & no & $\Vd$ headroom\\
Doppler FM & $\pi f_2V_{\mathrm{dem}}/(c\,\Sd)$, \eqref{eq:doppler} & yes ($\Sd$) & no & split; large $\Sd$\\
box air spring & $\frac{\gamma+1}{4}\frac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}}{\Vb}(1-\frac{\Qts^2}{\Qtc^2})$ & yes (box) & no & large $\Vb$; stuffing\\
flux modulation & $\propto i^2\,\dd\Le/\dd x$ & no & no & rings; low $\Le$\\
thermal AM & $-0.034\,\xi_P\Delta T_{\mathrm{rated}}$ dB & proxy ($\xi_P$) & no & $P$ headroom; split\\
creep/hysteresis & --- & no & no & suspension quality\\
linear response $H$ & --- & yes (TS) & \textbf{yes} & FIR (costs \eqref{eq:EQtax})\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{prop}[Distortion-referenced selection $=$ headroom maximization]
\label{prop:equivdist}
At fixed band, room, and target, every non-correctable component above is monotone
decreasing in the headroom vector
$h=(\Vd,\ \Sd,\ \Vb,\ \eta_0\Pmax)$. Hence sorting candidates by predicted
non-correctable distortion is the Pareto/lexicographic order on $h$: the v1 FOMs
are retained and \emph{reinterpreted as distortion predictors}; new relative to v1
are only the separate appearance of $\Sd$ (Doppler) and $\Vb$ (box spring), and the
sizing factor $1/\xi^\ast$ from the distortion budget.
\end{prop}

\noindent\textbf{Sorting recipe} (within a category fixed by nominal size $\Sd$
or by target $(L_t,\ \mathrm{room})$): compute
\[
\Big(\;\underbrace{d\,\xi_x(f_{\mathrm{pz}})}_{\mathrm{HD,\ IMD}},\quad
\underbrace{\pi f_2 X_1^{\max}/c}_{\mathrm{Doppler}},\quad
\underbrace{\tfrac{\gamma+1}{4}\tfrac{V_{\mathrm{dem}}}{\Vb}(1-\tfrac{\Qts^2}{\Qtc^2})}_{\mathrm{box}},\quad
\underbrace{0.034\,\xi_P\Delta T_{\mathrm{rated}}}_{\mathrm{thermal\ AM}}\;\Big)
\]
and rank by the dominant component (or lexicographically, largest first). Feasible
$\Leftrightarrow$ every component $\le$ its budget.

\section{Room closure: pressure zone, required $\Vd$, and the choice of $F_s$}
\label{sec:roomclose}

Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:distortion} priced every driver in free space
($r=1$\,m, A8). A real listening room changes the low-frequency boundary
condition itself once the acoustic wavelength exceeds the room's own
dimensions, and it does so for \emph{any} driver, sealed or vented alike; this
section makes that pressure-zone effect precise, turns it into the $\Vd$-sizing
and corner-placement rule actually used in Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure}, and gives
the abstract ``room'' a concrete face.

\subsection{Demand flattens below the pressure zone}

Lump the room (below its first axial mode) as an acoustic compliance
$C_R=V_{\mathrm{room}}/(\rho_0c^2)$ with leakage conductance $G_\ell$ ---
the standard low-frequency room lumped-element model \cite{beranek2012} ---
with source volume velocity $U=\Sd\dot x$:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{room}}=\frac{U}{j\omega C_R+G_\ell}
\ \xrightarrow[\ \omega\gg\omega_\ell\ ]{}\
\frac{\rho_0c^2\,\Sd x}{V_{\mathrm{room}}},
\qquad \omega_\ell=G_\ell/C_R,
\qquad f_{\mathrm{pz}}\simeq\frac{c}{2L_{\max}} .
\label{eq:pzdyn}
\end{equation}
Two structural consequences:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $p\propto x$ (not $\omega^2x$): the excursion demanded for a fixed target is
\emph{frequency-independent} below $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$. The total demand curve is
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{dem}}(f)=\sqrt2\,p_t\times
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2\pi r}{\rho_0\,(2\pi f)^2}\cdot C, & f\gtrsim f_{\mathrm{pz}}
\quad(\text{free-field/modal side, burst factor }C),\\[2ex]
\dfrac{V_{\mathrm{room}}}{\rho_0c^2}, & f\lesssim f_{\mathrm{pz}} ,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:demand}
\end{equation}
rising at 12 dB/oct down to $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ and \emph{flat} below. The two
branches agree at $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ up to $O(1)$: equating them gives
$V_{\mathrm{room}}\approx 2rL_{\max}^2/\pi$, which is a normal room at
$r\approx L/2$ --- the free-field and adiabatic pictures are the same physics
meeting at the transition.
\item The sealed roll-off ($-12$ dB/oct below $F_c$, at fixed drive
$x\to$ const) against the room term \eqref{eq:pzdyn} ($p\propto x$, flat) means:
a sealed system with $F_c\simeq f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ is flat \emph{in-room to the
leakage corner without any EQ}. This is the exact form of ``taper-off below $F_c$
compensated by room gain.'' Whether a vented system enjoys the same rescue is
exactly the question App.~\ref{sec:vented} answers (it does not: the $24$
dB/oct alignment collapses precisely the band this room term would have paid
for).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sizing rule and $F_s$ criterion}

\paragraph{A typical room.} To keep every number below concrete rather than
symbolic, fix one running example, used for the rest of this section, for
Appendix~\ref{sec:vented}, and for Appendix~\ref{app:room}: a rectangular room
$6.0\times4.0\times2.5$ m ($V_{\mathrm{room}}=60$ m$^3$, longest dimension
$L_{\max}=6$ m along the listening axis, soffit-mounted sources in the end wall,
A3), single fixed listening position (``the couch,'' A11) at
$r_{\mathrm{listen}}=3$ m from that wall. By \eqref{eq:pzdyn},
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}=c/2L_{\max}=28.6$ Hz. Choosing $r_{\mathrm{listen}}\approx
L_{\max}/2$ is not incidental: it is the condition, derived below
\eqref{eq:demand}, under which the free-field and pressure-zone demand branches
meet at $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ with no discontinuity (Fig.~\ref{fig:demand}) --- a
seat much closer to or farther from the source than $L_{\max}/2$ would still be
handled by \eqref{eq:demand}, but the two branches would then cross away from
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ and the simple corner rule \eqref{eq:Fsrule} below would need
the free-field branch evaluated at the true $r_{\mathrm{listen}}$ instead of the
room-volume shortcut. Real rooms of this size and construction (masonry or
dry-wall on studs, one open doorway) typically leak below $f_\ell\sim$10--20 Hz,
below which \eqref{eq:pz} stops being a tight bound (\emph{Caveats}, below).

Combining \eqref{eq:demand} with the distortion cap $\xi^\ast$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\
\Vd\ \ge\ \frac{1}{\xi^\ast}\,\max\!\left\{
\frac{\sqrt2\,p_t\,V_{\mathrm{room}}}{\rho_0c^2},\ \
C\,\frac{\sqrt2\,p_t\,2\pi r}{\rho_0\,\omega_{\mathrm{pz}}^{2}}\right\}
\qquad\text{(peak sine basis: }C{=}1,\ \xi^\ast{=}1)
\ }
\label{eq:Vdreq}
\end{equation}
with the pressure-zone branch the robust one (A11). Numerically
($\xi^\ast=1$, $C=1$; multiply by $1/\xi^\ast$ for a distortion budget; the
middle row is the $60$ m$^3$ running example above):

\noindent The corner criterion follows from the alignment line
\eqref{eq:alignment}: placing $F_c$ at the transition,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ F_c\simeq f_{\mathrm{pz}}=\frac{c}{2L_{\max}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{F_s}{\Qts}\simeq\frac{f_{\mathrm{pz}}}{\Qtc^\ast}\ }
\label{eq:Fsrule}
\end{equation}
(6 m room, $\Qtc^\ast=0.55$: $F_s/\Qts\le52$ Hz; the corner rate is now selected by
the \emph{room}, not by taste). $F_c$ below $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ wastes nothing but
box volume; $F_c$ above it leaves a correctable sag $[f_{\mathrm{pz}},F_c]$ taxed
by \eqref{eq:EQtax} --- bounded and often acceptable, per \eqref{eq:PL0}. The two
mistunings are not equally cheap: undershoot's shelf,
$20\log_{10}(f_{\mathrm{pz}}/F_c)$ dB, comes out with a free EQ \emph{cut};
overshoot's sag needs a taxed \emph{boost} that also eats excursion headroom.
Since \eqref{eq:Fsrule}'s exact match can force $\Qtc^\ast$ past a comfortable
ringing budget (Prop.~\ref{prop:decay}: $M_p\le1\%\Leftrightarrow\Qtc\le0.61$),
the practical rule is to fix $\Qtc$ from that budget first and let $F_c$
undershoot $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ rather than chase exact alignment
(Sec.~\ref{sec:example}).

\emph{Caveats.} Real rooms leak: below $f_\ell=\omega_\ell/2\pi$ (construction
dependent, often 10--20 Hz in masonry rooms, higher in lightweight ones) the
realized response falls $6$ dB/oct \eqref{eq:pzdyn} and \eqref{eq:pz} is an upper
bound; measured room gain typically realizes $6$--$12$ dB/oct slope between
$f_\ell$ and $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$. The air itself is irrelevantly linear here
($p/P_0\sim10^{-4}$ at 115 dB).

\section{Selection procedure}
\label{sec:procedure}

Given: room ($V_{\mathrm{room}}$, $L_{\max}$), band top $f_2$, peak-equivalent
target $L_t$ at the listening position, distortion budgets
$D^\ast$ (HD) and $D^\ast_{\mathrm{IM}}$ (Doppler), damping target $\Qtc^\ast$
(e.g.\ 0.55, $M_p=0.1\%$), burst factor $C$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:shape}, take 2),
burst headroom $\kappa$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[0.] \textbf{Room closure:} $f_{\mathrm{pz}}=c/(2L_{\max})$; band bottom
$f_1\to f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ (below it demand is flat, \eqref{eq:demand});
$\xi^\ast=D^\ast/0.1$ (or $\sqrt{D^\ast/0.1}$ if odd-order dominated).
\item \textbf{Displacement/distortion (binds at $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$, covers all
$f<f_{\mathrm{pz}}$):} $\Vd\ge$ \eqref{eq:Vdreq}. Predicted
$D\approx d_2\xi_x+d_3\xi_x^2$ \eqref{eq:HDscale}.
\item \textbf{Dissipation:}
$\eta_0\Pmax\ \ge\ 10^{(L_t-112.2)/10}$ (referred to 1 m/$2\pi$), $\eta_0$ from
\eqref{eq:eta0}; thermal AM $\approx0.034\,\xi_P\Delta T_{\mathrm{rated}}$ dB
(Sec.~\ref{sec:otherdist}).
\item \textbf{Split (Doppler):} choose $f_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with
$\pi f_{\mathrm{sp}}X_1^{\max}/c\le D^\ast_{\mathrm{IM}}$ \eqref{eq:doppler};
verify the attack driver's own in-band index at $(f_{\mathrm{sp}},f_2)$.
\item \textbf{Corner/settling/box:} $\Qts\le\Qtc^\ast$;
$F_s/\Qts\simeq f_{\mathrm{pz}}/\Qtc^\ast$ (sub, \eqref{eq:Fsrule}) resp.\
$F_c\lesssim f_{\mathrm{sp}}$ (attack); $\Vb=\Vas/((\Qtc^\ast/\Qts)^2-1)$;
box distortion \eqref{eq:boxHD} $\le$ budget (sets minimum $\Vb$, stuffing);
EQ tax below $F_c$ bounded via \eqref{eq:EQtax}, \eqref{eq:PL0}.
\item \textbf{Regime map:} $f_x$ \eqref{eq:fx}, $\hat f_x$ \eqref{eq:fxburst};
verify $p_{\max}(\omega)$ \eqref{eq:master} $\ge$ target across the band.
\item \textbf{Validity:} $f_2\ll f_L=\Re/(2\pi\Le)$; $ka(f_2)\lesssim1$; same
$\Xmax$ (A7) and power-rating (A5) standards across candidates.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Ranking inside one role (fixed category: nominal size or target class):
lexicographic on predicted non-correctable distortion
(Prop.~\ref{prop:equivdist}) --- in practice: sub role $\to$ maximize
$\Vd$, tie-break on $\Sd$ (Doppler) and $\eta_0\Pmax$; attack role $\to$ maximize
$\eta_0\Pmax$, subject to its own $\xi_x(f_{\mathrm{sp}})$ and Doppler index,
tie-break on $f_L$ and shorting rings.

\begin{itemize}
\item S meets 110 dB down to $f=\sqrt{10^{(110-108.5)/20}/\Vd}=22.7$ Hz on sine;
thermal needs only $\eta_0 P\ge0.60$ W$_{\mathrm{ac}}$, i.e.\ 141 W $\ll$ 600 W
(Remark~\ref{rmk:target}: nowhere near thermally limited at the target).
Its ceiling above 32 Hz is 116 dB (thermal) --- irrelevant at home levels.
\item S \emph{cannot} serve as attack driver regardless of its thermal math:
$f_L=139$ Hz sits inside the attack band (mechanism 3), a $\Qtc=0.55$ alignment
needs 918 L (mechanism 2), and its $\sigma/2\pi=42$ Hz cannot be raised (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}).
\item M holds 117 dB sine at 80 Hz and 120.6 dB in band --- 7--10 dB of headroom over
target. But its \emph{transient} excursion ceiling at 70 Hz is
$114.7-20\log_{10}2=108.7$ dB peak-equivalent: a 110 dB kick at 70 Hz clips
excursion. $\Rightarrow$ split at $\ge$80 Hz, exactly the empirically chosen HPF.
The split frequency is set by the \emph{burst} penalty (Lemma~\ref{lem:shape}), not
by the sine math.
\item M in 38 L reaches $\Qtc=0.55$ with $F_c=79$ Hz $\approx f_1$: corner correctly

box-invariant meaning of ``fast'').

same Pareto front. One driver covering both roles would need
$\beta B^2\approx1.1$: excluded.

distortion than SPL.''

$4.3\%$. M's own in-band index ($X_1=3.6$ mm at 80 Hz, $f_2=250$ Hz): $0.8\%$.
The split buys $\sim\!3\times$ lower FM distortion at identical SPL.

small next to the driver's rating: two coherent units need $N^2=4\times$ less
power each for the same target (A9), and $F_c=25.5$ Hz sits close enough to

near its own bathtub minimum rather than deep below the corner. Hence thermal
AM is $\approx0.13$ dB for any music crest factor; negligible here, dominant
only if one coil served both bands.
\item \textbf{$F_s$ rule \eqref{eq:Fsrule}:} required $F_s/\Qts\le
f_{\mathrm{pz}}/0.55=52$ Hz; S has 42 Hz --- compliant; at $\Qtc=0.61$ in 473 L,
$F_c=25.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ undershoots $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ by $3.1$ Hz: a
$0.99$~dB in-room shelf between $F_c$ and $f_{\mathrm{pz}}$, trimmed by a free
EQ cut. $\Qtc=0.61$ is chosen from the ringing budget (Prop.~\ref{prop:decay}:
$M_p\le1\%$) rather than from exact alignment, per Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\noindent The problem posed in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} was: given a room, one
listening position, and a target level, choose sealed bass driver(s) that
reach it with the least non-correctable distortion. The derivation of
Secs.~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:example} answers it as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two regimes, two drivers.} A sealed driver's output near $F_s$
is capped by displaced volume $\Vd=\Sd\Xmax$ (Cor.~\ref{cor:onlyVd}); above
$f_x$ \eqref{eq:fx} it is capped instead by dissipation $\eta_0\Pmax$
\eqref{eq:SPLT}. The motor materials bound (Thm.~\ref{thm:bound}) proves
$EBP\cdot u^2\le K_{\mathrm{mat}}\beta_{\max}$: stroke and corner rate trade
off through the same coil-mass fraction, so no single voice-coil motor
maximizes both $\Vd$ and $\eta_0\Pmax$ at once. A sub role (maximize $\Vd$)
and an attack role (maximize $\eta_0\Pmax$) are therefore best served by two
physically different drivers, split at a frequency set jointly by Doppler
\eqref{eq:doppler} and burst-excursion headroom (Sec.~\ref{sec:example}).
\item \textbf{Sealed, not vented.} Appendix~\ref{sec:vented} shows a vented
alignment's low-frequency asymptote is $24$~dB/oct, not sealed's $12$
(Prop.~\ref{prop:vented24}); that below tuning its excursion is bounded only
by drive, not by the box (Prop.~\ref{prop:ventedxmax}); and that reaching a
realistic in-room target at typical port sizes drives port air velocity well
past the empirical turbulence-onset threshold \eqref{eq:vport}. None of
these are amplifier, alignment, or execution details -- they follow from the
same lumped model \eqref{eq:ventedode} already validated for the sealed
case (Cor.~\ref{cor:portshut}). A sealed box forfeits at most an octave of
extension for a given volume, and recovers most of it for free from the
room (next point) -- a trade this document takes.
\item \textbf{Room gain closes the low end.} Below
$f_{\mathrm{pz}}=c/2L_{\max}$ the demand curve flattens \eqref{eq:demand},
and a sealed corner placed at $F_c\simeq f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ \eqref{eq:Fsrule}
is flat in-room to the leakage corner with no EQ (Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}).
A vented box's steeper $24$~dB/oct roll-off is not rescued the same way
(Appendix~\ref{sec:vented}): the room-gain argument is itself a reason to
prefer sealed, independent of points 1--2.
\item \textbf{The selection procedure is datasheet-only.} Every quantity in
Sec.~\ref{sec:procedure} -- $\Vd$, $\eta_0\Pmax$, $\sigma$, $f_L$, the
$F_s$/room rule, the distortion budget $\xi^\ast$ -- is computable from the
ten datasheet rows of Sec.~\ref{sec:assumptions}, with no free parameter
beyond the room, the target level, and the distortion budget themselves.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Verdict.} An optimal sealed bass monitor for one listening
position, in a room large enough to matter, is a \emph{two-driver} system --
a high-$\Vd$, long-throw driver (or driver pair/manifold) sealed and
corner-tuned to the room's pressure zone, plus a light, strongly motored
driver above the split -- sharing a common baffle plane (a soffit wall). No
single driver saturates both invariants at once (point 1), and no enclosure
other than sealed lets the room pay for the last octave without the costs
quantified in Appendix~\ref{sec:vented} (point 2).

\subsection*{Relation to previous work}

Sec.~\ref{sec:prevwork}'s review is closed by the results above: $EBP$
remains a legitimate enclosure-\emph{type} screen (it is exactly the
motor/mass ratio of Sec.~\ref{sec:materials}), but does not extend to an
absolute performance ranking -- the excursion regime's ranking is
$\Vd=\Sd\Xmax$ alone ($EBP$ enters only through $f_x$ and the corner rate),
and the dissipation regime's ranking is $\eta_0\Pmax$ \eqref{eq:SPLT}. The
overshoot, excursion-slope, and ``$\Qtc$-and-frequency'' shorthands
reviewed there are corrected, not overturned, by
Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:time}.

\subsection*{Limitations}

The model is LTI to leading order in $x/\Xmax$ (A1, A10) and excludes
directivity/breakup above $ka\approx1$ (A3); distortion laws are
order-of-magnitude ranking predictors, not $\pm1$~dB THD forecasts; and the
reference implementation fixes several deployment choices (half-space
radiation, one listening position, mono per-channel target level) rather
than exploring the general design space. None of these change the criteria
themselves -- only the constants that feed them. Appendix~\ref{sec:limits}
records the complete list.

\section{Why sealed, not vented}
\label{sec:vented}

Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose} showed that a sealed system with $F_c\simeq
f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ tapers off below its corner \emph{for free}: the room pays the
bill. A vented (ported) box promises to avoid the taper-off altogether,
extending flat output roughly an octave lower than a sealed box of the same
volume, with one driver instead of two. This section asks, rather than
asserts, what that promise costs -- reusing the free-air cone equation
\eqref{eq:ode} and the box-invariance of $\sigma$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance})
already established, and adding only the port as two new lumped elements.

\subsection{Minimal vented-box model}
\label{sec:vented-model}

Repackage \eqref{eq:newton}'s box-stiffness term as an acoustic compliance
$C_{ab}=\Vb/(\rho_0c^2)$, and add a port of acoustic mass and resistance
$M_{ap},R_{ap}$ driven by the box pressure $P_b$:
\begin{equation}
\Mms\ddot x+\Rms\dot x+\frac{1}{\Cms}x-\Sd P_b=\Bl i,
\qquad
C_{ab}\dot P_b=-\Sd\dot x-U_p,
\qquad
M_{ap}\dot U_p+R_{ap}U_p=P_b,
\label{eq:ventedode}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the \emph{free-air} cone (no box stiffness folded in here -- unlike
\eqref{eq:newton}, the box now talks to the cone only through $P_b$) loaded by
a second-order Helmholtz side-branch. In steady sine ($s=j\omega$, as in
\eqref{eq:xofe}), define the port$+$box polynomial
$D_p(s)=C_{ab}M_{ap}s^2+C_{ab}R_{ap}s+1$ (roots at $\omega_b=1/\sqrt{M_{ap}C_{ab}}$
when $R_{ap}=0$: the port tuning $F_b$). Eliminating $P_b,U_p$ from
\eqref{eq:ventedode} gives a 4th-order system --- the classical vented
alignment \cite{small1973vented}, written in \eqref{eq:ode}'s own notation so
every sealed-box quantity ($\sigma,\ws,\Cms$) transfers unchanged:
\begin{equation}
X(s)=\frac{\Bl}{\Re}E\,\frac{D_p(s)}{\Delta(s)},
\qquad
\Delta(s)=L(s)D_p(s)+\Sd^2s\big(M_{ap}s+R_{ap}\big),
\qquad
L(s)=\Mms\big(s^2+\sigma s+\ws^2\big),
\label{eq:ventedX}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{tot}}(s)=\Sd sX(s)+U_{\mathrm{port}}(s),
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{tot}}(s)=\Sd sX(s)\,\frac{D_p(s)-1}{D_p(s)},
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{port}}(s)=-\frac{\Sd sX(s)}{D_p(s)}.
\label{eq:ventedU}
\end{equation}

\begin{cor}[Consistency: a shut port is a sealed box]
\label{cor:portshut}
As $M_{ap},R_{ap}\to\infty$ at fixed ratio (infinite inertance blocks all
flow), $s(M_{ap}s+R_{ap})/D_p(s)\to1/C_{ab}$, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta(s)}{D_p(s)}\ \longrightarrow\ L(s)+\frac{\Sd^2}{C_{ab}}
=\Mms\big(s^2+\sigma s+\wc^2\big),
\label{eq:portshut}
\end{equation}
exactly \eqref{eq:ode}'s sealed operator, since $\Sd^2/C_{ab}\equiv
\rho_0c^2\Sd^2/\Vb$ is \eqref{eq:newton}'s box-stiffness term. The vented model
\emph{specializes} to the already-validated sealed one; it adds physics rather
than replacing it (numerically confirmed to $4$ significant figures for the
worked-example driver S).
\end{cor}

\subsection{Low-frequency asymptotics: two propositions the vented literature
states but rarely proves from the driver's own datasheet}
\label{sec:vented-asym}

\begin{prop}[$24$ dB/octave, not $12$]
\label{prop:vented24}
$\Delta(0)=\Mms\ws^2\ne0$ is finite ($D_p(0)=1$ in \eqref{eq:ventedX}), so as
$s\to0$, $U_{\mathrm{tot}}(s)\propto s^2(M_{ap}s+R_{ap})$. For an ideal port
($R_{ap}=0$): $U_{\mathrm{tot}}\propto\omega^3$, hence
$p\propto\omega\,U_{\mathrm{tot}}\propto\omega^4$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \text{vented low-}f\text{ slope}=24\ \mathrm{dB/oct}\ }
\qquad\text{vs.\ sealed's } 12\ \mathrm{dB/oct}\ \text{(Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}).}
\label{eq:vented24}
\end{equation}
(Confirmed numerically to exponent $4.0\pm0.1$ over two octaves below $F_b$ for
driver S, Fig.~\ref{fig:vented}.) A real port has $R_{ap}>0$:
$U_{\mathrm{tot}}\propto R_{ap}s^2+M_{ap}s^3$ crosses from resistive
($\propto\omega^2$, $18$ dB/oct in pressure) to inertive ($\propto\omega^3$,
$24$ dB/oct) at $\omega^\ast=R_{ap}/M_{ap}$: for any port built with reasonably
low loss ($R_{ap}\ll M_{ap}\omega_b$, i.e.\ a high port $Q$), $\omega^\ast\ll
\omega_b$ and \eqref{eq:vented24} governs essentially the whole sub-tuning band
of interest, not just an asymptotic tail.
\end{prop}

\begin{prop}[Excursion is not box-limited]
\label{prop:ventedxmax}
$\Delta(0)=\Mms\ws^2$ contains no $C_{ab},M_{ap},R_{ap}$ at all, so
\begin{equation}
X(0)=\frac{\Bl}{\Re}E\,\frac{D_p(0)}{\Delta(0)}=\frac{\Bl\Cms E}{\Re},
\label{eq:ventedx0}
\end{equation}
independent of box or port sizing: below tuning, cone excursion relaxes to the
driver's own \emph{free-air} (no enclosure at all) DC limit, not some
box-conditioned value (confirmed numerically to $<1\%$, Fig.~\ref{fig:vented}
bottom panel). This is the rigorous content of ``a vented box needs a subsonic
filter'': below $F_b$ the box supplies \emph{no} restoring force, and
excursion is bounded only by how much drive $E$ happens to be applied --
unlike sealed, where \eqref{eq:ode}'s $\wc^2$ (box included) bounds $x$ at
every frequency down to DC.
\end{prop}

\begin{prop}[Relative excursion at fixed drive]
\label{prop:ventedratio}
Same driver, same box volume $\Vb$ (hence same $\alpha=\Vas/\Vb$), same drive
$E$: sealed DC excursion is $X_{\mathrm{sealed}}(0)=\Bl\Cms E/(\Re(1+\alpha))$
(\eqref{eq:ode} at $s=0$), so
\begin{equation}
\frac{X_{\mathrm{vented}}(0)}{X_{\mathrm{sealed}}(0)}=1+\alpha=
\Big(\frac{F_c}{F_s}\Big)^2
\label{eq:ventedratio}
\end{equation}
(confirmed numerically to $0.1\%$) --- a clean reuse of the already-defined
$\alpha$, not a new constant. A vented alignment moves $(1+\alpha)\times$
farther, at identical sub-corner drive, than the same driver sealed in the
same box: for the worked example's $\Qtc=0.61$ box, $1+\alpha\approx1.63$,
roughly $60\%$ farther.
\end{prop}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig_vented_comparison}
\caption{Driver S, identical $473$~L box, sealed ($\Qtc=0.61$) vs.\ vented

\end{figure}

\subsection{Room integration: a bill the room cannot pay}
\label{sec:vented-room}

Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose} is not driver-specific: \emph{any} sealed system with
$F_c\simeq f_{\mathrm{pz}}$ gets its bottom octave for free because $-12$
dB/oct fixed-drive excursion roll-off matches $p\propto x$ (the room's own flat
pressure-zone term) to within $O(1)$ \eqref{eq:Fsrule}. A vented system's
$-24$ dB/oct (Prop.~\ref{prop:vented24}) is twice as steep in dB and is not
matched the same way: closing the extra $-12$ dB/oct with FIR would apply
\eqref{eq:EQtax}'s power tax to a driver whose excursion
(Prop.~\ref{prop:ventedxmax}) the box no longer limits \emph{at all} below
tuning --- the correction buys back level by driving an already
excursion-unbounded cone harder, with no $\Xmax$-derived ceiling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:freq}) to say when to stop. Sealed trades a taper-off the room
fills in for free; vented trades the same octave for a drive-side liability
the room can no longer absorb.

\subsection{Port air velocity and turbulence}
\label{sec:vented-port}

Near tuning, $D_p(j\omega_b)\to0$ for a low-loss port (by construction), so
$X(j\omega_b)\to0$ --- the classical cone-null --- and \eqref{eq:ventedU} gives
$U_{\mathrm{port}}(j\omega_b)=U_{\mathrm{tot}}(j\omega_b)$ \emph{exactly} (the
cone contributes nothing at its own null): essentially all output passes
through the port. Rewriting the monopole relation used throughout (A3,
\eqref{eq:pofx}) in terms of volume velocity $U=\Sd\dot x$,
$\hat p(\omega)=\rho_0\omega\hat U/(2\pi r)$, and evaluating at $\omega_b$ with
$U_{\mathrm{tot}}\approx U_{\mathrm{port}}=v_{\mathrm{port}}S_{\mathrm{port}}$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ v_{\mathrm{port}}\approx\frac{2\pi r\,p_t}{\rho_0\,\omega_b\,
S_{\mathrm{port}}}\ }
\label{eq:vport}
\end{equation}
Turbulence (``chuffing'') onset at $v_{\max}\approx17$ m/s is an
\emph{empirical} threshold \cite{dickason2006}, cited as an anchor exactly as
IEC~62458's $10\%$-distortion threshold anchors $\Xmax$ (A7) --- not
re-derived, and not universal (port flare and cross-section change the onset
somewhat \cite{beranek2012}), but the right order of magnitude for a plain
cylindrical port. For the running $60$~m$^3$/$110$~dB/$F_b=23$~Hz example
(Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose}):

\noindent A single reasonably sized ($10$~cm) round port already runs at
$\sim\!5\times$ the turbulence-onset velocity; holding $v\le v_{\max}$ at this
target needs a $\approx23$~cm port (or several smaller ones in parallel) ---

\subsection*{Passive radiators}

A passive radiator -- a driver-like cone with no voice coil or motor,
tuned by its own moving mass and suspension in place of the port's air
mass -- is a common alternative that avoids the mechanism above entirely:
no air moves through a constriction, so \eqref{eq:vport} and the
turbulence threshold simply do not apply, and several pro-audio monitors
use exactly this solution. It does not, however, change the underlying
alignment: a passive radiator is still driven by box pressure through a
fourth-order mass-spring-damper standing in for the port, so
Prop.~\ref{prop:vented24} and Prop.~\ref{prop:ventedxmax} (the steeper
roll-off and the box-unbounded excursion below tuning) and the missing
room-gain match of Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose} all still apply unchanged. And the
radiator is itself an added driver: once a second driver is accepted at
all, an \emph{active} one in the array proposed here is the better use of
it, contributing shared power and $\Vd$ (A9) that a passive radiator,
lacking its own voice coil, cannot provide. Room gain remains the
strongest of the reasons above -- it is the one advantage no port or
radiator geometry can reproduce.

\subsection{Summary: two regimes, two optimal drivers, or one compromise}
\label{sec:vented-summary}

Collecting Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:vented-port}: the excursion regime
wants high $\Vd=\Sd\Xmax$ (large cone, long throw); the dissipation regime
wants high $\eta_0\Pmax$ (strong motor, light cone, Sec.~\ref{sec:invariants}).
Corollary~\ref{cor:onlyVd} and Prop.~\ref{prop:thermal} show these are
different, weakly correlated optimization targets, so one driver rarely excels
at both (Sec.~\ref{sec:example}'s worked pair: $\beta\approx0.3$ each against
the same materials bound, Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}, with a single
both-in-one driver needing $\beta B^2\approx1.1$ -- excluded). A vented
alignment is, in this light, an attempt to cover the excursion regime's job
with a dissipation-regime driver, by moving the box's own resonance in as
extra gain: Secs.~\ref{sec:vented-model}--\ref{sec:vented-asym} showed this is
not free --- $24$ instead of $12$ dB/oct, an excursion no longer bounded by the
box at all below tuning, and a room term (Sec.~\ref{sec:vented-room}) that can
no longer absorb the difference --- and Sec.~\ref{sec:vented-port} showed the
mechanism that pays for the extra octave (port air velocity) is itself a
nonlinear, level-dependent distortion and noise source outside this document's
LTI accounting (A1, A10). Sealed keeps the two regimes, and the two drivers
that serve them, honestly separated; the rest of this document, and the
reference implementation (App.~\ref{sec:tool-scope}), is written for that
choice.

\section{Model limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

(i) $\Le(f)$ is semi-inductive (eddy losses); $f_L$ is a soft corner --- for ranking
use same-standard $\Le$@1 kHz, or measured $|Z|$. (ii) Power ratings: AES/IEC/
``program'' differ by up to $2\times$ (A5); power compression raises $\Re$ by
$+0.39\%/\mathrm{K}$ (Cu): $\Delta T=100$ K $\Rightarrow\eta_0\Pmax$ ceiling
$-1.4$ dB --- second-order for selection, first-order for sizing $\kappa$.
(iii) $\Xmax$ definitions vary; FOM 1 is linear in it (A7). (iv) Above
$ka\approx1$ ($287$ Hz for 18'', $428$ Hz for 12'') directivity and breakup
invalidate A3 --- outside the bands considered. (v) Box losses lower the realized
$\Qtc$ slightly ($Q_L\sim5$--$20$); \eqref{eq:alignment} is the lossless limit.
(vi) Suspension creep and $\Cms(x)$ shift $F_c$ by several \%; irrelevant to
ranking. (vii) Multiple subs / manifold: A9 scaling, plus boundary gain not modeled.
(viii) Distortion laws (Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}) are leading-order with $O(1)$
weights (A10); published Klippel scans supersede them when available --- use
\eqref{eq:HDscale}--\eqref{eq:boxHD} for ranking and sizing, not for absolute THD.
(ix) Cone breakup is \emph{linear} and hence FIR-correctable on axis only; off-axis
the correction fails (directivity error). Keeping each band below breakup remains a
hard constraint that no DSP relaxes.

\subsection*{Scope of the reference implementation}
\label{sec:tool-scope}

The companion Python tool (\texttt{audioshape}) implements
Secs.~\ref{sec:distortion}--\ref{sec:procedure} against a driver database, but
fixes several free choices to a single concrete deployment rather than
exploring the general design space; these are recorded here so paper and code
stay in sync:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{Half-space radiation only.} Every driver/role uses the
  $2\pi$ (soffit-wall) constants of \eqref{eq:SPLL}; free-standing/$4\pi$
  placements are out of scope.
\item[(b)] \textbf{One fixed listening position, DSP-aligned.} The tool
  targets a single ``couch'' point and assumes a linear-phase/time/EQ DSP
  alignment across drivers, so only the \emph{non-correctable} (nonlinear)
  distortion of Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion} is scored --- never distortion an
  FIR filter could remove (Sec.~\ref{sec:distortion}, ``What LTI correction
  can and cannot do'').
\item[(c)] \textbf{Room gain via the pressure-zone corner only.} Below
  $f_{\mathrm{pz}}=c/2L_{\max}$ the demand curve flattens per
  Sec.~\ref{sec:roomclose} and App.~\ref{app:room}; no other room-mode modeling is
  attempted.
\item[(d)] \textbf{Two independent roles, no cross-coupling term.} A
  \emph{sub} role covers $[F_{\mathrm{low}},F_{\mathrm{split}}]$ and an
  \emph{attack} role covers $[F_{\mathrm{split}},F_{\mathrm{high}}]$, each
  evaluated with its own excursion demand and its own self-Doppler reference
  tone (Sec.~\ref{sec:doppler}) at the top of \emph{its own} band. Because the
  two roles are physically separate enclosures (e.g.\ a shared mono manifold
  for the sub vs.\ a stereo tower for the attack driver) sharing only the
  baffle plane, no acoustic coupling term between them is modeled.
\item[(e)] \textbf{Mono target level, no automatic stereo summing credit.}
  $\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{target}}$ is defined as the level required from one
  source/channel alone. A single shared mono sub manifold must reach it
  directly; each channel of a genuinely stereo attack/tower must also reach it
  \emph{on its own}, with no $+3$ to $+6$ dB summing credit assumed for
  correlated/decorrelated stereo bass (that credit would only apply to an
  architecture using two independent mono subs, which is not what is modeled
  here).
\item[(f)] \textbf{Configuration is a recipe file, not a parameter sweep.}
  Room size, listening distance, target SPL, distortion budget and role
  bands are fixed per run by a hand-authored recipe; the tool ranks and pairs
  drivers for that one design point rather than optimizing over the general
  scenario space.
\end{enumerate}

These are deliberate simplifications, not limitations of the underlying
math: relaxing any of (a)--(f) only changes which constants feed
Secs.~\ref{sec:freq}--\ref{sec:roomclose}, not the criteria themselves.

\section{Room pressure zone: the below-corner floor}
\label{app:room}

Rigid sealed room, volume $V_{\mathrm{room}}$, frequencies below the first mode
($f<c/2L_{\max}$): adiabatic compression gives the flat, frequency-independent bound
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{pz}}=\rho_0c^2\,\frac{\Sd x}{V_{\mathrm{room}}}
\ \Rightarrow\
\mathrm{SPL}_{\mathrm{pz,max}}
=20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\rho_0c^2\,\Vd}{\sqrt{2}\,V_{\mathrm{room}}\,p_0}\right).
\label{eq:pz}
\end{equation}
Driver S in 60 m$^3$: $105.6$ dB \emph{flat to DC} at $\Xmax$, while the 1-m
free-field line \eqref{eq:SPLL} has fallen to $105.6$ dB already at
$17.7$ Hz. Real rooms leak; \eqref{eq:pz} is an upper bound approached below the
leakage corner --- this is the quantitative form of ``sealed keeps going below $F_c$
and the room lifts it,'' the mechanism a vented alignment's steeper $24$ dB/oct
collapse cannot use the same way (Sec.~\ref{sec:vented-room}).
